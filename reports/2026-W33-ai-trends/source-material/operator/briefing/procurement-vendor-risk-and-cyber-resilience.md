## Facts and observations

- Health-sector cybersecurity materials frame cyber resilience as patient-safety and operational-continuity work, not only IT hygiene.
- Third-party and vendor dependencies are a recurring healthcare risk surface.
- AI procurement should consider data rights, model behavior, transparency, monitoring, incident response, contract obligations, and cybersecurity readiness.
- NIST AI RMF provides vocabulary for mapping, measuring, managing, and governing AI risks.
- These sources support procurement and risk-management discipline; they do not prove that any vendor's AI product is safe, compliant, resilient, or effective.

## W33 framing

- Reader-safe headline: healthcare AI risk increasingly sits in vendor governance and cyber resilience.
- Treat AI buying decisions as ongoing risk-management decisions, not one-time software adoption.
- Connect contract, monitoring, incident response, data rights, and cybersecurity controls.

## Evidence limits

- Cybersecurity and risk-framework materials are governance references.
- They do not establish vendor performance, organizational maturity, or product compliance.
