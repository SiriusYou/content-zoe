## Facts and observations

- FDA maintains a public AI/ML-enabled medical-device page and frames device AI as software requiring regulatory attention.
- FDA's predetermined change control plan guidance addresses how certain AI-enabled device software modifications can be described and bounded in marketing submissions.
- Good machine learning practice principles emphasize multi-disciplinary expertise, clinically relevant data, performance evaluation, and human-AI team considerations.
- These sources support lifecycle governance: model changes, monitoring, validation boundaries, intended use, and postmarket learning matter.
- These sources apply to regulated device software and should not be generalized to all healthcare workflow AI.

## W33 framing

- Reader-safe headline: regulated AI-enabled device software shows why evidence lifecycle matters.
- Use FDA materials as an example of change-control and monitoring discipline.
- Contrast device oversight with broader workflow AI, where governance may be less formal or less visible.

## Evidence limits

- FDA device references establish regulatory and lifecycle concepts.
- They do not validate non-device AI tools, prove organization-level readiness, or establish that deployed AI improves outcomes.
