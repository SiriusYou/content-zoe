## Facts and observations

- HHS frames AI implementation alongside public trust, governance, data infrastructure, and responsible use.
- HHS health-technology leadership materials connect data liquidity, affordability, interoperability, and an AI-enabled healthcare system.
- HealthIT.gov materials connect certification updates, information sharing, and algorithm transparency.
- TEFCA materials describe national exchange infrastructure and interoperability direction.
- These sources support the idea that AI governance depends on data and transparency controls, not just model selection.

## W33 framing

- Reader-safe headline: AI governance depends on data infrastructure that can support transparency and accountability.
- Discuss interoperability and algorithm transparency as control surfaces.
- Avoid implying that data exchange volume or certification activity proves AI safety, fairness, adoption quality, or ROI.

## Evidence limits

- Federal infrastructure and certification materials support direction, control surfaces, and policy context.
- They do not prove clinical performance, implementation quality, or product-level validation.
