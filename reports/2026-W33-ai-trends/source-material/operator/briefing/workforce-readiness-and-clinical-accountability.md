## Facts and observations

- AMA's augmented-intelligence framing treats AI as support for physicians and care teams, not a replacement for professional judgment.
- AMA survey material supports the claim that professional AI exposure is broad enough to create training and governance pressure.
- AMA materials keep human oversight, workflow fit, patient communication, trust, and skill-maintenance concerns in view.
- These sources support a workforce-readiness story: adoption attention should translate into training, escalation pathways, review practices, and accountability structures.
- These sources do not prove clinical outcome improvement, productivity ROI, audited deployment quality, or patient safety.

## W33 framing

- Reader-safe headline: healthcare AI adoption pressure is becoming a workforce-readiness problem.
- Connect use signals to training, documentation, escalation, review, and human accountability.
- Avoid treating self-reported use as proof that AI is deployed safely or improving care.

## Evidence limits

- Survey and professional-association materials are useful for exposure, sentiment, and governance priorities.
- They are not clinical trials, implementation audits, or proof of product-level effectiveness.
