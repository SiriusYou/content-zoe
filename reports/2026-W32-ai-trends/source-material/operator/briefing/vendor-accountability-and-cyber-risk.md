## Facts and observations

- HSCC's third-party AI risk guide frames healthcare AI vendor dependence as a supply-chain transparency and procurement-control issue.
- HSCC materials connect third-party AI risk to contract terms, accountability, disclosure, performance expectations, data rights, and cybersecurity governance.
- FDA medical-device cybersecurity guidance connects connected-device risk to design, labeling, premarket submissions, and quality-system considerations.
- Third-party AI can create operational risk even when the AI tool is not a regulated medical device.
- These sources support governance and risk-management framing, not proof that organizations have implemented the recommended controls.

## W32 framing

- Reader-safe headline: vendor accountability is becoming a core healthcare-AI control surface.
- Treat third-party AI as procurement, cyber, privacy, and operational-resilience risk.
- Avoid claiming that guidance publication equals implementation maturity.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support risk framing and control categories.
- They do not quantify maturity, vendor performance, or control adoption.
