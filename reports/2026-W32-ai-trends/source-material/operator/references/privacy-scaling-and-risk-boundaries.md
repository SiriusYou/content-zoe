## Facts and observations

- OECD frames scaling AI in health as a responsible-scaling and policy-readiness challenge.
- WHO health-AI regulatory materials emphasize privacy, security, transparency, documentation, data quality, lifecycle tracking, and risk management.
- HIPAA remains central for covered entities and business associates, but it does not cover every health-adjacent AI or non-covered data context.
- HHS de-identification guidance shows why de-identified data claims depend on method, context, and controls.
- NIST AI RMF provides general risk-management vocabulary; it is not a health-specific deployment-quality metric.

## W32 framing

- Reader-safe headline: responsible scaling requires risk boundaries, not just broader deployment.
- Use OECD and WHO to frame policy readiness and regulatory considerations.
- Use HIPAA, de-identification, and NIST as boundary-setting references, not as product-validation evidence.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support governance and reader-boundary claims.
- They do not establish deployment scale, clinical effectiveness, vendor performance, or legal compliance for any specific organization.
