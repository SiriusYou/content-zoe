## Facts and observations

- HIPAA privacy obligations are central for covered entities and business associates, but HIPAA does not cover every health-adjacent consumer AI product, data broker, vendor workflow, or non-covered data context.
- HHS de-identification guidance matters because AI-related claims about de-identified data should be tied to method, context, and governance rather than to the label alone.
- FDA's medical-device cybersecurity guidance connects connected device risk to design, labeling, and premarket submission considerations.
- NIST AI RMF language helps organize governance, mapping, measurement, and management of AI risk, but does not substitute for health-specific evidence or legal analysis.

## W30 framing

- Reader-safe headline: privacy, cybersecurity, and risk management remain gating issues for health AI because healthcare data and connected systems carry high consequence.
- Do not imply that HIPAA, de-identification, FDA guidance, or NIST language alone proves a deployed AI system is safe.
- Tie safety and privacy claims to the specific context: covered-entity status, data type, intended use, device status, and governance controls.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support reader cautions and governance framing.
- They do not establish deployment scale, clinical effectiveness, product performance, or legal compliance for any specific vendor.
