## Facts and observations

- HSCC's 2026 third-party AI guide addresses AI supply-chain transparency and discovery/disclosure gaps that make healthcare AI vendor risk difficult to manage.
- HSCC frames third-party AI risk as a health-sector cybersecurity and governance issue involving accountability, transparency, contracting, performance expectations, and vendor dependence.
- The guidance preview links AI cybersecurity guidance themes to governance, third-party risk, education, enablement, and shared responsibility with vendors.
- Third-party AI risk is broader than software security. It also affects procurement, contract language, data ownership, model use, monitoring, auditability, and escalation.
- HSCC is an industry advisory/cybersecurity working group source. It supports governance and risk-management framing, not proof of adoption scale or implementation quality.

## W30 framing

- Reader-safe headline: health-system AI governance is moving toward vendor-risk and procurement controls because AI systems increasingly arrive through third-party tools and embedded workflows.
- Emphasize that non-device AI can still create patient-safety, privacy, cybersecurity, and accountability exposure.
- Avoid claiming that health systems have implemented these controls uniformly.

## Evidence limits

- The sources support transparency, procurement, and third-party risk claims.
- They do not quantify how many organizations have mature AI governance programs or prove that particular vendors meet the recommended controls.
