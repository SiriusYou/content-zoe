## Facts and observations

- HHS's March 2026 leadership announcement frames health technology work around data liquidity, affordability, interoperability, and an AI-enabled health care system.
- HHS describes a return of department-wide technology roles under OCIO while sharpening ONC's focus on nationwide health IT interoperability and data liquidity.
- HHS's TEFCA announcement ties national interoperability progress to broader technology and AI coordination, while the underlying claim is about records exchange and infrastructure, not AI clinical outcomes.
- HHS AI strategy material frames governance, public trust, and responsible implementation as part of federal AI work.
- FDA's AI-enabled device software draft guidance gives lifecycle-management and marketing-submission recommendations for devices with AI-enabled software functions.
- FDA's digital-health guidance list distinguishes AI-enabled device software materials, predetermined-change-control planning, and other digital-health policy work.

## W30 framing

- Reader-safe headline: U.S. policy signals are converging on infrastructure and lifecycle controls -- interoperability, data liquidity, governance, and regulated-device evidence -- rather than a single unified AI rule.
- Distinguish regulated AI-enabled medical-device software from certified health IT, administrative AI, and general workflow tools.
- Treat TEFCA and HHS leadership changes as infrastructure/governance context, not proof of mature AI deployment.

## Evidence limits

- Policy and guidance sources show direction, coordination, and expected control surfaces.
- They do not prove deployed AI tools improve care, lower costs, or meet clinical safety thresholds in every setting.
