## Facts and observations

- OECD's 2026 health-AI work frames responsible scaling as a policy-readiness challenge, not merely a technology-availability problem.
- OECD points to urgency around responsible scaling and cross-system alignment for health AI.
- WHO's health-AI regulatory considerations emphasize privacy, security, transparency, documentation, lifecycle tracking, data quality, and risk management.
- WHO's materials support a regulatory-readiness and safeguards frame, not a claim that health AI is already mature or uniformly safe.
- NIST's AI Risk Management Framework provides cross-sector vocabulary for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks. It is useful for structuring governance language, but it is not health-specific proof of deployment quality.

## W30 framing

- Reader-safe headline: international sources are pushing the same direction as U.S. governance sources -- scale requires evidence, transparency, lifecycle controls, and risk management.
- Use OECD and WHO as policy-readiness and regulatory-consideration anchors.
- Use NIST as general risk-management vocabulary, not as a healthcare deployment metric.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support responsible-scaling and governance claims.
- They do not prove patient outcomes, country-level readiness, organizational maturity, or product performance.
