## Facts and observations

- AMA reports that 81 percent of physicians use AI professionally in 2026, more than double the 2023 share.
- AMA says the 2026 survey added physician perspectives on patient use of AI tools and physician training needs, including concern about potential skill loss as adoption grows.
- AMA uses "augmented intelligence" to emphasize AI's assistive role and the goal of enhancing, not replacing, physician judgment.
- The survey supports claims about physician exposure, use, sentiment, training needs, and workflow pressure.
- The survey does not prove audited deployment scale, clinical effectiveness, equity, or patient-outcome improvement.

## W30 framing

- Reader-safe headline: physician use is now common enough that training, clinical judgment, workflow fit, and governance are operational issues rather than speculative future concerns.
- Treat self-reported physician use as adoption and sentiment evidence only.
- Connect physician training needs to governance maturity, not to claims that AI is clinically validated.

## Evidence limits

- AMA survey evidence is self-reported and should be framed as perception/use evidence.
- It does not establish that any specific AI tool is safe, effective, independently validated, or outcome-improving.
