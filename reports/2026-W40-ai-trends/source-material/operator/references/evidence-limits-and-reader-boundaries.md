# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this file to constrain claims. The W40 report should describe measurement as a necessary operating discipline, not as proof that healthcare AI is mature, safe, fair, secure, or effective.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF, WHO health AI governance guidance, AMA AI evaluation materials, ONC/ASTP HTI-1 transparency materials, FDA AI-enabled device guidance, CHAI assurance guidance, AHRQ patient-safety concepts, Joint Commission responsible health-data materials, and HHS cybersecurity guidance are all governance-oriented sources for this report.
- These sources support evaluation questions, monitoring duties, transparency requirements, and accountability concepts.
- They do not provide local deployment evidence for the W40 report.

## Supported claims

- Measurement can make governance reviewable by linking claims to evidence, thresholds, and decisions.
- Reader-facing reporting should distinguish governance guidance from deployment proof.
- A mature measurement program needs evidence, owners, thresholds, and response options.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim measured clinical outcomes, cost savings, compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, fairness, safety, or patient trust.
- Do not cite runtime metadata, `.runs`, transcripts, run-state files, DB rows, or repo-internal files as reader evidence.
- Do not use stale week keys, prior-week titles, or previous report prose as source material.
- Do not convert governance guidance into proof of outcomes.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources define boundaries for safe interpretation. They do not provide direct evidence of real-world performance, legal compliance, safety, fairness, privacy protection, or patient trust.
