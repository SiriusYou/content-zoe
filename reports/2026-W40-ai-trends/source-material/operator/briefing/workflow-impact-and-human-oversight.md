# Workflow impact and human oversight

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep human oversight concrete. The report should argue that oversight is measurable only when workflows show who sees AI output, how much context they receive, whether they have time and authority to act, and how concerns are escalated.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AMA augmented-intelligence principles frame AI as assistive and emphasize physician responsibility, transparency, and workflow-sensitive deployment.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports human oversight, accountability, and protection from harm.
- AHRQ patient-safety and learning-health-system concepts support feedback, event review, and improvement routines.
- NIST AI RMF supports assigning roles and monitoring risk in use context.

## Supported claims

- Human oversight is an operating condition, not only a governance label.
- Useful oversight evidence can include training completion, escalation logs, override patterns, workflow burden, alert fatigue reports, and review outcomes.
- Oversight should account for how AI changes staff work, patient communication, and decision timing.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a human-in-the-loop design eliminates risk.
- Do not imply clinicians can meaningfully oversee a system without usable context and authority.
- Do not claim workflow impact has been measured unless staged evidence says so.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support workflow-aware oversight as a governance requirement. They do not prove that any organization has effective oversight in practice.
