# Accountability records and response routines

## Editorial use

Use this file to close the report around accountable response. Measurement is weak if it produces observations that no one owns. The report should emphasize governance records that show who reviewed evidence, what decision was made, and how the organization responded.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF supports governance roles, risk documentation, measurement, management, and lifecycle accountability.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports accountability and responsiveness to people affected by AI.
- AHRQ patient-safety and learning-health-system materials support event review, near-miss learning, and improvement routines.
- Joint Commission responsible health-data materials support governance structures, patient transparency, data controls, and algorithm validation in secondary-use contexts.

## Supported claims

- Accountability records should connect evidence to decisions, not just collect artifacts.
- Response routines can include escalation, user retraining, workflow clarification, vendor inquiry, narrower use, monitoring changes, incident review, or tool pause.
- Governance is more credible when it can show how concerns travel from signal to review to decision.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim response routines prevent harm.
- Do not infer an incident occurred.
- Do not say accountability records prove trust, safety, or compliance.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support accountable response routines as governance infrastructure. They do not measure their effectiveness in a real deployment.
