# Change control and release evidence

## Editorial use

Use this file to tie AI governance to release discipline. Healthcare AI systems can change through model updates, data-pipeline changes, interface changes, vendor releases, workflow redesign, or policy updates. The report should frame change control as evidence that the organization knows what changed, why, and what was checked.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- FDA predetermined change control plan guidance for AI-enabled device software functions supports planned modifications, validation methodology, impact assessment, monitoring, and lifecycle evidence in regulated device contexts.
- NIST AI RMF supports documentation, governance, measurement, and risk response over time.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports lifecycle assurance and evidence review.
- HHS 405(d) and HC3 materials support operational resilience and third-party awareness where vendor or system changes affect healthcare operations.

## Supported claims

- Change-control records should identify the change, affected workflow, expected benefit, known risk, validation method, rollback or pause criteria, and accountable owner.
- Vendor releases and data-pipeline changes should not be treated as invisible technical maintenance when they can affect clinical or operational decisions.
- Release evidence is stronger when it connects monitoring results to a decision: continue, limit, retrain, update, pause, or retire.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a change plan proves safety or effectiveness.
- Do not imply FDA PCCP concepts govern non-device operational AI.
- Do not claim vendor updates are safe or unsafe without staged evidence.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support change control as an operating evidence duty. They do not prove that any specific release was safe, effective, or compliant.
