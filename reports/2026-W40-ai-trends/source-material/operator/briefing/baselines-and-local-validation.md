# Baselines and local validation

## Editorial use

Use this file to anchor the report in the need for local baselines. A healthcare AI tool can be supported by external studies or regulatory documentation while still requiring local validation against the population, workflow, and decision context where it is used.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF supports mapping context, measuring risk, and managing risks across the lifecycle.
- AMA AI evaluation materials emphasize clinical relevance, transparency, patient safety, and physician understanding when evaluating AI applications.
- FDA AI-enabled device guidance supports evidence, validation, and change-control concepts in regulated device contexts.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports context-sensitive deployment, human oversight, and accountability.

## Supported claims

- Baselines should describe what the tool is expected to do, for whom, in which workflow, and under what limits.
- Local validation should look at patient mix, input quality, workflow handoffs, escalation paths, and how people use or override outputs.
- External evidence can inform evaluation, but local deployment still needs a record of fit and limits.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim local validation proves safety, fairness, or effectiveness.
- Do not imply every healthcare AI tool is a regulated medical device.
- Do not say external studies transfer cleanly to a local deployment.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support local baselines and validation as governance duties. They do not provide local performance measurements for a specific deployment.
