# W34 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W34-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W34
Prepared: 2026-05-24

## Intended angle

This source set supports a broad healthcare-AI weekly report for a content product. The strongest angle is evidence operations: healthcare AI is becoming less about whether organizations are interested in AI and more about whether they can operate the evidence, review, procurement, privacy, and resilience systems that responsible AI requires.

The report title should be a reader-facing topic title, not a job label. Do not include `2026-W34` or any other week key in the H1 title. Week identity belongs in job metadata and README, not in the article headline.

## Source classes included

- Physician use, augmented-intelligence framing, training pressure, and human-accountability references.
- FDA AI-enabled medical-device software references for lifecycle control, performance monitoring, and change-management discipline.
- HHS, ONC, TEFCA, and HealthIT.gov references for data infrastructure, algorithm transparency, and governance control surfaces.
- Health-sector cybersecurity and third-party risk references for operational resilience and procurement.
- HIPAA, de-identification, NIST, OECD, and WHO references for privacy and governance boundaries.

## Source classes not included

- No private project documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No patient-level data.
- No vendor scorecard or procurement benchmark.
- No private market, funding, or litigation map.
- No direct clinical outcome dataset.

## Claims that are safe to make

- AI interest and use signals are strong enough that healthcare organizations need evidence operations, not just pilots.
- Workforce readiness, human review, escalation, and documentation remain central to clinical AI accountability.
- FDA device AI materials illustrate why lifecycle control and change management are important, while remaining limited to regulated device software.
- Interoperability and algorithm-transparency infrastructure can make AI easier to review, but cannot prove product performance.
- Cybersecurity, third-party risk, and procurement controls are part of healthcare AI governance because AI systems depend on vendors, data flows, contracts, and operational continuity.
- Privacy and governance frameworks are boundaries and vocabulary, not proof of local deployment quality.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI broadly improves patient outcomes or lowers costs.
- That professional use or vendor adoption proves audited deployment quality.
- That federal AI strategy or interoperability activity proves any specific AI system is safe, fair, or effective.
- That FDA device software discipline applies unchanged to every healthcare workflow AI tool.
- That HIPAA, de-identification, NIST, OECD, or WHO references prove compliance or readiness.
- That cyber risk is solved by vendor language or by adopting a named framework.

## Publication gate guidance

Keep evidence warnings visible. Reject or restage if the generated report overstates clinical outcomes, ROI, implementation maturity, vendor validation, regulatory coverage, cyber resilience, privacy compliance, or international readiness. Also reject or edit if the H1 title includes a week key such as `2026-W34`; the W33 convention is week-token-free H1s.
