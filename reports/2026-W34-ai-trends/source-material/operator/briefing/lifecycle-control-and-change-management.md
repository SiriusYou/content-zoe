## Facts and observations

- FDA AI/ML-enabled medical-device materials provide a concrete regulated example of software lifecycle oversight.
- Predetermined change control planning highlights the need to define and bound future AI-enabled software modifications.
- Good machine learning practice principles emphasize relevant data, performance evaluation, intended use, and human-AI team considerations.
- These references support lifecycle control and change-management framing.
- They do not prove that non-device workflow AI has comparable oversight or that regulated AI broadly improves outcomes.

## W34 framing

- Reader-safe headline: AI accountability depends on lifecycle controls, not one-time launch decisions.
- Use device software as an example of why change management and monitoring matter.
- Keep the boundary clear: device regulation is not a blanket validation of all healthcare AI.

## Evidence limits

- FDA references are regulatory and lifecycle references for device software.
- They are not evidence of sector-wide governance maturity or non-device product performance.
