## Facts and observations

- AMA materials frame augmented intelligence as support for clinicians and care teams, not replacement of professional judgment.
- AMA survey material supports a claim of broad professional AI exposure and rising governance pressure.
- Training, workflow integration, patient communication, skill maintenance, and human oversight remain explicit concerns.
- These materials support an evidence-operations story: exposure creates demand for review routines, documentation, escalation paths, and quality checks.
- These materials do not prove audited deployment quality, clinical benefit, productivity ROI, or patient-safety improvement.

## W34 framing

- Reader-safe headline: the next AI question for healthcare is whether organizations can operate review and accountability routines at scale.
- Connect professional exposure to training, auditability, escalation, documentation, and human responsibility.
- Avoid treating self-reported use as evidence of safe implementation.

## Evidence limits

- Professional-association and survey materials are useful for exposure, sentiment, and governance priorities.
- They are not outcome studies or implementation audits.
