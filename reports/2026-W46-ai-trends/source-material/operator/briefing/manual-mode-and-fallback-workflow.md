# Manual mode and fallback workflow

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the fallback concrete. The report should ask what staff do when the AI-enabled workflow is unavailable or untrusted: where they get information, which work continues, which work pauses, who approves substitutions, and how the organization reconciles work after normal operation resumes.

## Source basis

- ONC SAFER Guides are designed as self-assessment tools for safety and safe use of EHRs, including contingency planning and clinical process areas.
- ONC selecting/upgrading health IT materials describe contingency planning as preparation for clinicians and support staff to minimize the impact of EHR outages.
- HHS Security Rule summary materials include continuity of critical business processes while protecting ePHI during emergency mode.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement supports local governance, validation, monitoring, and context-adapted use.

## Reader-safe claims

- Manual mode should name the substitute workflow, the owner, the trigger for switching, and the minimum information required to continue critical work.
- The plan should define which AI-supported tasks can be deferred, which require manual completion, and which require escalation because the fallback changes risk.
- Recovery should include reconciliation: notes, orders, messages, queues, alerts, and handoffs created during downtime may need review before the normal workflow is trusted again.
- Fallback communication matters. Staff and affected users need to know what changed, which workflow applies, and where to escalate confusion.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim paper, manual, or alternate digital workflows are safer than AI-enabled workflows.
- Do not imply fallback instructions are usable without training and practice.
- Do not claim a downtime plan is enough if reconciliation after recovery is undefined.
- Do not claim every AI workflow needs the same fallback depth.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support practical fallback questions. They do not show that any local staff can execute a manual workflow accurately, quickly, or safely under pressure.
