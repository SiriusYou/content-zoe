# Privacy, data governance, and patient trust

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep privacy and trust inside the operational governance frame. The report should connect data use, patient trust, de-identification limits, and transparency without implying that privacy compliance is proven.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- HHS OCR HIPAA and de-identification guidance supports careful treatment of protected health information and warns that de-identification is a method with assumptions and limits.
- ONC/HealthIT interoperability and transparency concepts support documentation of data origin, intended use, and user-facing context.
- WHO and OECD AI governance principles support transparency, accountability, privacy, and human rights as boundary conditions.
- NIST AI RMF supports mapping data context, measuring risks, and managing controls across the lifecycle.

## Supported claims

- Patient trust depends on whether organizations can explain what data is used, what the AI system is for, what it is not for, and who remains accountable.
- De-identification and privacy controls are operating disciplines, not a one-time declaration.
- Healthcare AI governance should document data provenance, access controls, use limitations, auditability, and patient-facing communication where appropriate.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a deployment is HIPAA-compliant.
- Do not imply de-identified data is always risk-free.
- Do not claim that transparency alone creates trust.

## Evidence warning

The report can say that privacy and data governance are necessary conditions for trust. It cannot claim that the sector has achieved trust or that any local deployment is compliant without direct evidence.
