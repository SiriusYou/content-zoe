# Topic and source boundary

## Intended reader-facing angle

The report should focus on this thesis:

Healthcare AI is moving from governance language toward measurable operating discipline. The important question is no longer only whether a tool has an AI policy, a transparency label, or a promising use case. The harder question is whether the organization can measure local behavior, validate workflow fit, define escalation, hold vendors to evidence duties, and preserve privacy/trust boundaries after deployment.

## Required style

- Do not include any week key in the H1 title.
- Use a title frame that differs from the recent "Next Test Is ...", "Assurance Phase", and "Show Its Work" frames.
- Keep the report source-bounded and evidence-cautious.
- Preserve explicit `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in both locales.
- Avoid promotional language.
- Avoid naming a specific vendor as better or worse.

## Safe claims

- Responsible healthcare AI is becoming more operational and measurement-oriented.
- Local validation, telemetry, escalation, procurement evidence, and privacy/data governance are connected responsibilities.
- Guidance and frameworks can sharpen the questions organizations ask, but they do not prove deployment quality.
- Human oversight must be designed as a workflow with roles, triggers, and documentation.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim clinical outcomes, cost savings, staffing improvements, compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, or vendor resilience are proven.
- Do not say every workflow assistant is a regulated medical device.
- Do not imply AI adoption equals maturity.
- Do not cite runtime metadata, transcripts, `.runs`, `run-state.json`, or repo-internal files as reader sources.

## Publication gate

Reject or restage if the draft:

- uses only job metadata or generated context as evidence;
- includes any week key in the H1;
- repeats the recent title templates;
- cites runtime metadata;
- overstates the sources into outcome proof;
- treats frameworks as proof of implementation;
- omits the evidence-limits posture.
