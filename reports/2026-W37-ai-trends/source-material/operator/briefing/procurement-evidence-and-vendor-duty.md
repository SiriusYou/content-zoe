# Procurement evidence and vendor duty

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect healthcare AI governance to procurement. The safe thesis is that buyers increasingly need evidence duties in contracts and implementation processes, not just model descriptions and product demos.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- HSCC guidance on third-party AI risk and health-sector cyber resilience supports supply-chain visibility, vendor accountability, incident response, and risk management.
- HHS 405(d) / HICP materials support the practical need for cybersecurity hygiene, risk management, and organizational responsibility in healthcare.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports asking vendors for evidence about intended use, performance, data, monitoring, and lifecycle processes.
- NIST AI RMF supports risk documentation, governance roles, measurement, and ongoing management.

## Supported claims

- AI procurement should ask what a vendor can document, what a customer can independently verify, and what happens when the system changes or fails.
- Evidence duties can include model-card style limitations, intended-use boundaries, monitoring responsibilities, update notification, incident communication, data-flow documentation, and exit plans.
- Procurement is part of governance because healthcare organizations often depend on external vendors for model behavior they cannot fully inspect.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim that contract language proves vendor resilience.
- Do not evaluate or rank specific vendors.
- Do not state that a named framework guarantees compliance, safety, or cybersecurity.

## Evidence warning

Use vendor evidence as a governance requirement, not as proof of vendor performance. The report should say that the sources support better questions for procurement, not verified answers about particular vendors.
