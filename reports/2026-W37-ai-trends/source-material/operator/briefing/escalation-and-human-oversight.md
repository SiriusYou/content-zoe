# Escalation and human oversight

## Editorial use

Use this file to emphasize that human oversight only matters when it has a real escalation path. The report should avoid generic "human in the loop" language and focus on observable responsibilities.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AMA physician-facing AI guidance supports physician responsibility, transparency, and the need for training and appropriate use.
- WHO ethics and governance guidance supports human autonomy, accountability, transparency, and inclusive governance.
- NIST AI RMF supports assigning roles and responsibilities, documenting risks, and maintaining risk controls.
- HHS and health-sector cyber/resilience materials support incident handling and operational responsibility when digital tools fail or behave unexpectedly.

## Supported claims

- Human oversight is credible only when a person or team knows when to review an output, when to override it, when to escalate it, and how to document the decision.
- Escalation should be designed before deployment, not invented during an incident.
- Healthcare AI governance needs clarity on responsibility: clinical owner, operational owner, data owner, vendor contact, privacy/security contact, and safety-review path.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim that human review automatically eliminates risk.
- Do not imply that a clinician can safely compensate for every model limitation.
- Do not turn accountability into blame; the stronger frame is system design and clear roles.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support escalation and role clarity as governance requirements. They do not prove that any deployment has adequate oversight or that human review is sufficient in practice.
