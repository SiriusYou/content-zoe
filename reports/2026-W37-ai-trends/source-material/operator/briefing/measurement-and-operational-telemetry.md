# Measurement and operational telemetry

## Editorial use

Use this file to frame the weekly report around measurement. The safe thesis is that healthcare AI adoption is becoming less credible unless organizations can measure how tools behave in real workflows after deployment. This is an operating claim, not a claim that AI tools already improve outcomes.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI Risk Management Framework: Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage create a practical vocabulary for moving from principles to repeatable risk work. The Measure function is useful for evaluation, monitoring, and feedback, but it is not clinical evidence by itself.
- AHRQ patient-safety materials and learning-health-system concepts support the idea that safety programs need event review, near-miss learning, and feedback loops. Apply this as a governance analogy for AI-enabled workflows.
- Joint Commission responsible AI/data governance materials support the need for accountable data practices, oversight routines, and quality discipline in health-system settings.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports a lifecycle view of AI governance: identify the use case, evaluate evidence, monitor performance, and maintain accountability after deployment.

## Supported claims

- Measurement is becoming part of responsible healthcare AI use because static approvals do not reveal how a tool behaves inside local workflows.
- Useful telemetry can include workflow impact, escalation frequency, user override patterns, complaint and incident review, model-update history, data drift signals, and documentation of human review.
- Measurement should be tied to decisions: keep, modify, restrict, retrain, pause, or retire a tool.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim that the staged sources prove improved patient outcomes, lower costs, reduced clinician burden, or sector-wide maturity.
- Do not imply that telemetry alone makes an AI system safe.
- Do not rank vendors or health systems.

## Evidence warning

Any report using this material should preserve a warning that measurement frameworks and governance routines are directional evidence only. They support what responsible oversight should look like; they do not prove that any local deployment is performing well.
