# Local validation and workflow fit

## Editorial use

Use this file to make a bounded point: healthcare AI evidence often travels poorly unless the receiving organization checks local workflow fit. A model or assistant may be promising in one setting but still require local review before it is trusted in another.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- FDA lifecycle and predetermined-change-control materials are strongest for regulated device software, but they illustrate a broader governance lesson: performance and risk can change when software, data, intended use, or operating conditions change.
- ONC/HealthIT decision-support transparency concepts support the idea that users need information about source attributes, intended use, and limitations to judge whether a recommendation is appropriate.
- AMA augmented-intelligence principles and physician-adoption survey material support the human-facing side: clinicians need training, review expectations, and trust-building evidence, not just tool availability.
- WHO and OECD AI governance materials support context, accountability, human oversight, and risk-based deployment.

## Supported claims

- Local validation is less about repeating every original study and more about checking whether the tool fits the actual workflow, population, staffing model, escalation route, and documentation environment.
- Healthcare AI governance should ask: Who is expected to rely on the output? What happens if the output is wrong? What local data or process changes could shift performance? How will concerns be reported?
- A tool can be transparent enough to inspect and still not be locally ready.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not say every healthcare AI tool must be regulated like medical-device software.
- Do not imply that clinician adoption statistics prove tool quality.
- Do not treat an implementation guide or transparency label as evidence of real-world performance.

## Evidence warning

The report can state that local validation is a credible governance requirement. It cannot state that the sector has solved local validation or that a specific tool is validated without local evidence.
