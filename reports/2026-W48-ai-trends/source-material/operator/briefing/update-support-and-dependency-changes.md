# Update, support, and dependency changes

## Editorial use

Use this file to cover what may have changed since launch. The report should ask whether the model, interface, data feed, EHR integration, vendor platform, identity service, support model, staff workflow, or local protocol changed enough to require renewed review.

## Source basis

- ONC implementing health IT materials emphasize configuration, validation, maintenance, interfaces, and the interaction among technology, people, processes, and organizational culture.
- ONC selecting/upgrading health IT materials describe technology selection and upgrading as opportunities to examine needs, workflows, and risks.
- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management and monitoring.
- AMA and WHO materials support attention to workflow, accountability, and human oversight.

## Reader-safe claims

- A tool may need renewed review even if the name is unchanged, because interfaces, data feeds, support teams, workflows, or vendor releases changed.
- Support history matters: unresolved tickets, degraded service, delayed notices, downtime, training gaps, or weak escalation paths can affect continued-use decisions.
- Dependency changes can shift risk without changing the visible model.
- Renewal should ask whether fallback, permissions, training, and monitoring still match the current workflow.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim every update is material.
- Do not claim vendor releases are unsafe by default.
- Do not infer local support quality without staged evidence.
- Do not claim maintenance records prove safety or compliance.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support questions about maintenance, updates, support, and dependencies. They do not include local architecture diagrams, support-ticket records, release notes, or outage evidence.
