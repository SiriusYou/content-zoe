# Actual use and monitoring record

## Editorial use

Use this file to make renewal evidence-based. The report should emphasize that a renewal decision should look at what actually happened during use: who used the tool, where it was used, what it affected, what concerns were raised, and what monitoring signals changed.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports monitoring, measurement, context-specific risk management, and response.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement points to local validation and monitoring adapted to organizational context.
- ONC DSI materials support structured visibility into decision-support interventions in covered contexts.
- WHO health AI guidance supports accountability, transparency, oversight, and protection of affected people.

## Reader-safe claims

- Renewal review can look at actual user groups, settings, patient or operational populations, override patterns, complaints, incident reviews, queue effects, downtime, handoffs, and workflow burden.
- Monitoring should compare actual use with approved use, not only output quality.
- Evidence gaps should be visible at renewal. If performance, safety, burden, user feedback, or monitoring evidence is missing, that absence should shape the decision.
- The review should document who examined the evidence and what decision followed.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not invent local metrics, incident rates, or complaint patterns.
- Do not claim monitoring prevents harm.
- Do not claim logs or dashboards prove benefit.
- Do not imply missing evidence automatically means the tool should be retired.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support monitoring and evidence-record questions. They do not provide local logs, dashboards, incident files, complaints, or measured outcomes.
