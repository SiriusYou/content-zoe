# Renewal review and continued use

## Editorial use

Use this file to make W48 about continued-use review. The report should argue that renewal is a governance checkpoint: the organization should compare the current workflow against the original approved use, evidence basis, training assumptions, monitoring plan, fallback plan, and ownership record.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management, context mapping, roles, monitoring, and response.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement supports policies, local validation, monitoring, and use adapted to organizational context.
- ONC health IT selection and upgrading material treats technology selection, implementation, and upgrading as moments for needs assessment and risk management.
- AMA augmented-intelligence materials connect AI use with workflow, training, accountability, privacy, cybersecurity, and professional responsibility.

## Reader-safe claims

- Renewal should ask whether the tool is still being used for the reviewed purpose, in the reviewed setting, by the reviewed users.
- Continued use should be checked against actual use, monitoring signals, support history, training state, scope changes, fallback readiness, and unresolved concerns.
- Renewal can produce several decisions: continue, narrow scope, pause, replace, retire, or reopen validation.
- A renewal checkpoint is useful because it forces governance to look at evidence from operation, not only evidence from launch.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim renewal review proves safe or effective use.
- Do not imply every tool needs the same renewal cadence.
- Do not claim continued use is inappropriate just because review is needed.
- Do not claim any named organization has implemented this process.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support continued-use questions. They do not include local renewal decisions, actual-use data, performance metrics, or outcome evidence.
