# Near-miss file and event scope

## Editorial use

Use this file to make W50 about a concrete near-miss and patient-safety event record. The report should argue that healthcare AI governance needs a place to capture events that did not become confirmed harm but still reveal unsafe conditions, workflow fragility, or correction needs.

## Source basis

- AHRQ patient-safety materials support event reporting, near-miss and unsafe-condition concepts, investigation, analysis, communication, and improvement.
- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management, mapping, measurement, management, monitoring, response, and documentation.
- WHO health-AI ethics material supports accountability, transparency, human oversight, protection, and inclusiveness.

## Reader-safe claims

- The near-miss file should define what belongs in scope before the first report arrives.
- Relevant AI workflow events can include unexpected outputs, wrong-context recommendations, delayed escalation, missed referral prompts, unsafe automation workarounds, handoff failures, downtime fallback failures, override spikes, queue delays, and cases caught before reaching the patient.
- A near miss should be treated as a learning signal, not as proof that harm occurred.
- Unsafe conditions can be worth capturing even when no patient was harmed and no clinician made an error.
- The record should identify where the signal came from: clinician report, staff report, monitoring alert, patient feedback, complaint path, audit, or technical log.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim any specific AI workflow generated a near miss.
- Do not claim a near-miss file proves safety culture or harm reduction.
- Do not define legal reporting thresholds.
- Do not suggest that every unusual output is a patient safety event.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support event-scope questions. They do not include local incident reports, near-miss logs, unsafe-condition records, patient harm findings, or root-cause analyses.
