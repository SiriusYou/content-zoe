# Corrective action and ownership

## Editorial use

Use this file to make the near-miss file operational. The report should end with ownership and action: a report that does not lead to a decision, mitigation, validation question, or follow-up owner is not enough.

## Source basis

- AHRQ event-response materials support investigation, analysis, learning, and improvement after safety events.
- NIST AI RMF supports governance, measurement, management, monitoring, response, documentation, and role assignment.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance supports governance, local validation, monitoring, and organizational context.
- ONC SAFER contingency planning supports continuity, downtime, recovery, and fallback planning.

## Reader-safe claims

- Corrective action should be tied to the specific failure mode or unresolved uncertainty.
- Possible actions include narrowing the workflow, adding a fallback, reopening validation, changing training, adjusting escalation, improving notices, fixing integration, asking for vendor information, monitoring a new signal, pausing use, or retiring the workflow.
- Ownership should name the accountable clinical, operational, quality, patient-safety, IT, data-governance, compliance, or vendor-management role as appropriate.
- The file should track whether follow-up actually happened and whether the risk was closed, reduced, accepted, or still unresolved.
- Repeated low-severity events can deserve review if they reveal the same pattern.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim corrective action proves the system became safe.
- Do not claim a committee review alone fixes risk.
- Do not claim all events require the same action or owner.
- Do not invent local remediation results.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support corrective-action and ownership questions. They do not include local remediation plans, committee minutes, vendor notices, validation results, patient outcomes, or proof that a mitigation worked.
