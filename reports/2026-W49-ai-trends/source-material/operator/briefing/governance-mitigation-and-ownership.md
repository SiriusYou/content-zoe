# Governance, mitigation, and ownership

## Editorial use

Use this file to turn the equity topic into an operating checkpoint. The report should avoid abstract fairness language and instead ask who owns the review, what evidence is examined, what mitigation is available, and what decision can be made when equity risk remains unresolved.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports governance, mapping, measuring, managing, roles, responsibilities, monitoring, and response.
- eCFR 45 CFR 92.210 provides source context for identifying and mitigating nondiscrimination risk in patient care decision support tools. Use as source context only, not legal advice.
- AMA augmented-intelligence material supports attention to workflow, accountability, training, privacy, cybersecurity, and professional responsibility.
- Joint Commission/CHAI and WHO sources support responsible adoption, governance, local context, transparency, oversight, and accountability.

## Reader-safe claims

- Equity review should end with a decision record, not only a statement of values.
- The record can continue the workflow, narrow scope, require monitoring, require mitigation, pause use, return to validation, create a fallback path, or assign a remediation owner.
- Mitigation should be tied to the specific risk observed or unresolved, such as unclear escalation, missing data, language-access friction, inaccessible notices, subgroup performance uncertainty, or extra documentation burden.
- Ownership should be named across clinical workflow, data governance, quality, compliance, IT, and operations as appropriate.
- Review should have triggers for earlier revisit, including model changes, data-feed changes, interface changes, setting changes, user changes, complaint patterns, override spikes, or access-burden signals.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim mitigation proves legal compliance or fairness.
- Do not claim a policy alone solves equity risk.
- Do not claim all tools require the same mitigation or governance structure.
- Do not give legal advice about civil rights, procurement, certification, FDA, HIPAA, or malpractice.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support governance and mitigation questions. They do not include local mitigation plans, legal review, policy documents, committee minutes, enforcement facts, or proof that a mitigation worked.
