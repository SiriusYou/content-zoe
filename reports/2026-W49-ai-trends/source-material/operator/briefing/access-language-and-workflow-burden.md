# Access, language, and workflow burden

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the report from reducing equity to model outputs. The report should show that an AI workflow can shift burden through scheduling, portals, forms, referrals, triage queues, documentation, language access, disability access, and escalation paths.

## Source basis

- WHO health-AI ethics material supports inclusiveness, equity, human oversight, transparency, and protection of people affected by AI systems.
- AMA augmented-intelligence material supports attention to physician workflow, patient relationship, professional responsibility, privacy, cybersecurity, and implementation concerns.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance supports organizational governance, local validation, monitoring, and responsible adoption.
- HHS/OCR Section 1557 material provides nondiscrimination context. Do not turn that context into legal advice.

## Reader-safe claims

- A model can produce the same score while the surrounding workflow creates different burden for different patients.
- Equity review should examine language access, disability accommodation, health literacy, digital access, portal dependence, transportation constraints, and caregiver reliance when those factors shape the AI-enabled pathway.
- Burden can show up as extra forms, longer delays, unclear notices, harder appeals, missed callbacks, more manual documentation, or fewer usable fallback paths.
- Staff burden matters too because uneven documentation, escalation, or override work can change which patients receive timely review.
- The review should ask whether a human can understand, override, pause, or route around the workflow when the tool does not fit a patient's situation.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim any named AI workflow creates access discrimination.
- Do not claim language access or disability access obligations are fully described by the staged material.
- Do not claim workflow review proves patient trust or outcome improvement.
- Do not substitute general equity language for concrete workflow questions.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support access-burden and workflow questions. They do not include patient interviews, accessibility testing, language-service records, complaint files, appeal files, or measured delays.
