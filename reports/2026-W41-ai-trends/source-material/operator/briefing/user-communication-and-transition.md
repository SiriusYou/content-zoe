# User communication and transition

## Editorial use

Use this file to make lifecycle changes user-facing. If an AI-mediated workflow is limited, paused, retired, or replaced, clinicians, operational staff, and sometimes patients may need to know what changed, what workflow to use instead, and where to raise concerns.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AMA augmented-intelligence principles support transparency, physician responsibility, and workflow-aware implementation.
- WHO health AI guidance supports transparency, accountability, and respect for people affected by AI.
- AHRQ patient-safety concepts support communication, learning, and improvement routines.
- ONC/ASTP transparency concepts support contextual information around predictive decision support in certified health IT settings.

## Supported claims

- Transition communication should explain what changed, who is affected, what fallback or replacement workflow applies, and how concerns are handled.
- Clinician-facing communication should include practical workflow instructions, not only governance rationale.
- Patient-facing communication may be relevant when an AI-mediated workflow meaningfully affects care, access, or data use.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim users or patients understand a change because a notice exists.
- Do not imply every lifecycle change requires patient notification.
- Do not say a transition improved safety, trust, or outcomes without staged evidence.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support communication as part of accountable lifecycle management. They do not measure whether users understood or benefited from any transition.
