# Pause and rollback criteria

## Editorial use

Use this file to frame off-ramps as part of governance. Monitoring only matters if the organization has named actions when signals move. Pause and rollback criteria make the response side of oversight visible.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF supports ongoing risk measurement, management, and response.
- FDA predetermined change control and lifecycle concepts support bounded modifications, validation, monitoring, and response in regulated device software contexts.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports human oversight, accountability, and protection from harm.
- AHRQ patient-safety concepts support learning, escalation, and improvement routines.

## Supported claims

- A governance program should define what conditions trigger review, narrower use, rollback, pause, or retirement.
- Pause criteria can include unresolved safety concerns, changed workflow context, missing evidence, unstable data inputs, unreviewed vendor changes, or repeated user concerns.
- Rollback planning should identify what prior workflow or tool state is restored and who authorizes the change.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a specific tool should be paused.
- Do not claim pause criteria prevent harm.
- Do not imply FDA change-control concepts apply to all non-device AI workflows.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support pause and rollback criteria as governance tools. They do not prove that any deployment has met a pause threshold or that rollback would improve outcomes.
