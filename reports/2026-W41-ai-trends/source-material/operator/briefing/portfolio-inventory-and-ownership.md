# Portfolio inventory and ownership

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect lifecycle decisions to the AI portfolio. An organization cannot pause, limit, retire, or replace what it cannot identify. The report should emphasize inventory, status, owner, intended use, evidence state, monitoring state, and review cadence.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF supports governance roles, risk documentation, inventory-like risk mapping, and lifecycle management.
- Joint Commission responsible health-data materials support governance structures, data controls, algorithm validation, and patient transparency in secondary-use contexts.
- ONC/ASTP transparency concepts support source attributes and disclosure for predictive decision support in certified health IT.
- WHO health AI guidance supports accountability and transparency.

## Supported claims

- A portfolio inventory should identify active, limited, suspended, retired, and experimental tools.
- Ownership records should name who is responsible for evidence review, monitoring, change approval, user communication, and retirement decisions.
- Portfolio review helps avoid hidden AI use, unmanaged pilots, and tools that remain active after evidence or context changes.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim an organization has a complete inventory without staged evidence.
- Do not equate an inventory with safe or effective governance.
- Do not imply all predictive decision support is covered by the same regulatory or certification regime.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support AI portfolio inventory and ownership as governance duties. They do not prove that any organization has a complete or effective inventory.
