# Retirement and replacement evidence

## Editorial use

Use this file to make retirement normal rather than exceptional. Healthcare AI tools can become misaligned because populations, workflows, evidence, model behavior, vendor support, or organizational priorities change. The report should treat retirement as an accountable lifecycle decision.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management and documentation of decisions.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports lifecycle assurance, monitoring, and evidence review.
- AMA AI evaluation materials support clinical relevance, transparency, and physician accountability when evaluating tools.
- WHO health AI guidance supports accountability and context-sensitive deployment.

## Supported claims

- Retirement evidence should explain why a tool is no longer fit for use, what evidence was reviewed, what replacement or fallback exists, and how users are informed.
- Replacement should not be treated as automatic improvement; a new tool needs its own baseline, validation, workflow fit, and monitoring plan.
- Keeping an obsolete tool active can be a governance risk, but the report should not infer that any specific tool is obsolete.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a retired or replacement tool is safer or better without evidence.
- Do not imply retirement is always the correct response.
- Do not say a sector-wide wave of retirement is underway unless staged evidence supports it.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support retirement and replacement evidence as lifecycle-governance concepts. They do not provide examples of a specific tool being retired or replaced.
