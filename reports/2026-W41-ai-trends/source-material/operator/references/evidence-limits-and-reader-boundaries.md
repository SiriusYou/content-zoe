# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this file to constrain claims. The W41 report should describe lifecycle off-ramps as necessary governance infrastructure, not proof that healthcare AI programs are mature or safe.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- NIST AI RMF, WHO health AI governance guidance, AMA augmented-intelligence materials, ONC/ASTP transparency materials, FDA AI-enabled device lifecycle guidance, CHAI assurance guidance, AHRQ patient-safety concepts, Joint Commission responsible health-data materials, and HHS cybersecurity guidance are governance-oriented sources for this report.
- These sources support lifecycle questions, ownership, monitoring, resilience, and accountability concepts.
- They do not provide local deployment evidence for the W41 report.

## Supported claims

- Lifecycle governance should include launch controls and exit controls.
- A normal governance vocabulary should include active, limited, paused, retired, and replaced states.
- Reader-facing reporting should distinguish governance guidance from deployment proof.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim measured clinical outcomes, cost savings, compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, fairness, safety, effectiveness, or patient trust.
- Do not cite runtime metadata, `.runs`, transcripts, run-state files, DB rows, or repo-internal files as reader evidence.
- Do not use stale week keys, prior-week titles, or previous report prose as source material.
- Do not convert governance guidance into proof of outcomes or maturity.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources define boundaries for safe interpretation. They do not provide direct evidence of real-world performance, legal compliance, operational maturity, safety, fairness, privacy protection, or patient trust.
