# Research Brief: Healthcare AI Needs Exit Controls

## Core angle

Healthcare AI governance is incomplete without off-ramps. The report should argue that once an organization defines baselines, monitoring, and accountability, it also needs practical ways to limit, pause, roll back, retire, or replace an AI-mediated workflow when evidence, context, vendor support, or operational fit changes.

## What the draft can say

- Off-ramp criteria make monitoring operational because they connect signals to named actions.
- Portfolio inventory and ownership records help organizations know which tools are active, limited, suspended, retired, or experimental, and who is responsible for review and transition decisions.
- Retirement should be framed as a normal lifecycle decision, not as proof that a tool failed.
- Replacement is not automatic improvement; any successor tool still needs its own baseline, validation, workflow fit, and monitoring plan.
- Vendor exit planning is part of resilience because support, hosted services, data feeds, and handoffs can affect operations even if a model is unchanged.
- Transition communication should explain what changed, who is affected, what fallback or replacement workflow applies, and how concerns are handled.

## Recommended structure

1. Open with the thesis that launch controls are not enough; healthcare AI also needs exit controls.
2. Explain the off-ramp set: limit, pause, rollback, retire, replace.
3. Tie off-ramps to portfolio inventory and named ownership.
4. Show why retirement and replacement need documented evidence rather than assumption.
5. Close on resilience and user communication: vendor exit, fallback workflows, and concern-routing.

## Evidence posture

- Keep the piece source-bounded and governance-focused.
- Preserve explicit `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in both locales.
- Use a clean H1 with no week key.
- Distinguish governance guidance from deployment proof in every section.

## Do not claim

- No clinical outcomes, cost savings, compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, fairness, safety, effectiveness, or patient trust.
- No incidents, drift events, vendor failures, or proof that any specific tool should be paused, retired, or replaced.
- No claim that having policies, inventories, or contract language proves mature execution.

## Source gap to state plainly if needed

The staged material supports lifecycle-governance concepts only. It does not provide deployment-specific evidence, outcome data, or named case examples showing that a healthcare organization successfully executed a pause, rollback, retirement, replacement, or vendor exit.
