# Documentation, feedback, and monitoring

## Why workarounds should become visible signal

Workarounds are often informal. Users may bypass the output, double-check it elsewhere, or reinterpret it without documenting the reason. If those adaptations stay invisible, governance teams may miss evidence that the workflow is not fitting real work.

The article may connect workaround documentation to lifecycle governance. A workaround log can create a way to notice friction, output misfit, missing context, unclear ownership, fallback dependence, or a need to review the use boundary.

## Documentation questions

- What workaround was observed?
- Where did it occur?
- Who was the intended user?
- What output was involved?
- What reason category was reported, if any?
- What next action was affected?
- Who reviews repeated patterns?
- When does the pattern trigger review?

These questions can make workaround signals more actionable. They do not prove that the workflow is safe, unsafe, effective, ineffective, compliant, or mature.

## Monitoring boundary

Workaround counts, bypass patterns, duplicate-work reports, and user feedback can support review questions when those data exist. They should not be invented. The article should not claim measured rates, patterns, outcomes, or incidents.
