# Source attributes and local context

## Why context depends on the workflow

The meaning of a workaround depends on local workflow context. Bypassing an output in one setting may mean the output arrived too late. In another setting it may mean the user needed missing context, a fallback path, or another professional review. The intended user, output purpose, downstream action, source attributes, owner group, monitoring responsibility, and escalation route all affect interpretation.

The article may say that available governance sources support disciplined questions about workflow fit, documentation, monitoring, contingency planning, and escalation. It must also say that those sources do not prove any local workaround pattern is complete, correctly captured, safe, unsafe, compliant, noncompliant, effective, fair, mature, or outcome-changing.

## Local context that may change

- clinical or operational setting;
- intended user;
- output purpose;
- downstream action influenced by the output;
- reason category for the workaround;
- source attributes available to reviewers;
- owner group;
- monitoring responsibility;
- fallback route;
- escalation route.

The article should use these examples to explain why workaround signals can be useful, not to imply that every local record contains these fields.

## Public language guard

When describing evidence limits, use reader-facing terms such as "available governance sources", "the source basis", "local evidence", and "review record". Do not point readers toward non-reader operational details.

When naming the source basis for readers, use public source-family names. Evidence-limit language should focus on what the source basis can and cannot prove.
