# Owner, review, and next action

## Why a workaround signal needs an owner

A workaround log is weaker if no one owns follow-up. If repeated bypasses, duplicate work, or informal reinterpretations are visible but do not reach an owner group, the signal can become another dashboard field that no one acts on.

The article may connect workaround visibility to owner-of-record thinking. The owner does not prove the workflow is safe or unsafe. The owner makes it clearer who can review the signal, request evidence, clarify the use boundary, adjust fallback, or escalate unresolved questions.

## Next-action categories the article may discuss

- clarify the intended use;
- gather evidence;
- review workflow fit;
- review monitoring signals;
- adjust user-facing context;
- revise fallback route;
- contact the owner group;
- escalate unresolved questions;
- trigger governance review;
- narrow, pause, or retire a particular use.

These are governance routing categories, not clinical instructions or legal determinations. They should not be described as proof of safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, or maturity.

## Boundary

The presence of a next action does not prove that the organization has solved the issue. It can make the workaround signal easier to route and review.
