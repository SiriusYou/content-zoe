# Non-use, bypass, and shadow workflows

## Why non-use matters

Non-use can be a governance signal. If the intended user repeatedly ignores an output, waits for another source, or acts only after recreating the work manually, the organization may need to ask whether the output is understandable, timely, trusted, relevant, or connected to the right action.

The article should avoid treating non-use as automatic resistance or automatic evidence of failure. The source-bounded claim is narrower: non-use deserves enough visibility to ask what it means.

## Workaround categories the article may discuss

- bypassing the AI-enabled output;
- duplicating the work in another tool;
- delaying action until another person checks it;
- reinterpreting a score or flag informally;
- using a non-AI path;
- copying the output into a different workflow without context;
- relying on peer confirmation before acting;
- ignoring the output after repeated mismatch.

These are workflow examples, not universal clinical instructions. The article should not imply that every setting has the same workaround pattern or that any local behavior is legally required by the source basis.

## Important boundary

Do not present workaround behavior as inherently good or inherently bad. The source-bounded claim is that visible workaround patterns can help organizations decide what needs review, clarification, redesign, fallback, or escalation.
