# Unknown, invisible, and normalized workarounds

## Unknown behavior

Unknown workaround behavior should remain visible as a review question. If no one knows whether users bypass, duplicate, delay, reinterpret, or avoid an output, the organization cannot assume the workflow is fitting real work. The next action may be observation, feedback design, monitoring review, owner confirmation, or escalation.

The article should frame unknown behavior as a governance visibility problem, not as proof that the workflow is unsafe or noncompliant.

## Invisible behavior

Workarounds can remain invisible when they happen outside the system of record, in side conversations, in duplicated notes, or in separate tools. The source-bounded claim is not that any specific organization has invisible workarounds. The claim is that invisible behavior can make governance signals harder to see.

## Normalized behavior

Some workarounds become normal. Users may no longer see them as worth reporting. A normalized workaround should not be converted into proof that the workflow works. It should remain available as a question: why did this adaptation become necessary, and what should be reviewed?

The article may connect this point to disagreement-path and handoff-context thinking from prior themes, but it should keep the primary focus on workaround signals.
