# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Evidence posture

The source basis supports a cautious, governance-oriented article about workarounds as signals in healthcare AI workflows. It can explain why documentation, workflow fit, human oversight, monitoring, escalation, contingency planning, decision-support visibility, responsible adoption, and bounded device context make bypasses, duplicate work, non-use, and informal reinterpretation important to notice.

The source basis does not prove that any named organization has a workaround-monitoring process. It does not supply local workaround logs, bypass counts, owner rosters, committee charters, audit trails, utilization data, model-performance measurements, safety outcomes, equity outcomes, legal conclusions, or named implementation proof.

The article should therefore frame workaround visibility as a monitoring and governance discipline, not as proof that an AI-enabled workflow is safe, unsafe, compliant, noncompliant, effective, ineffective, fair, unfair, trusted, untrusted, mature, or outcome-changing.

## Required warning posture

Use explicit evidence-limit warnings in both locales. The warnings should say that the article is limited to source-bounded governance, health IT, decision-support, workflow documentation, safety-management, responsible-adoption, and bounded device-context material. They should also state that the article does not include local workaround logs, bypass counts, committee charters, audit logs, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.

Warning language must be locale-pure. English comments should contain English only. Chinese comments should contain Chinese only.

Warnings should not describe how evidence was prepared. Keep the warning focused on what the source basis can and cannot prove.

## Reader questions the article may answer

- Why can workarounds and non-use matter for healthcare AI governance?
- What can go wrong if informal bypasses stay invisible?
- What fields make a workaround log operationally useful?
- How do owner group, monitoring contact, fallback route, escalation route, and review trigger fit together?
- Why should normalized or invisible workaround behavior remain a review question?
- What can the available governance sources support, and what can they not prove?

## Reader questions the article must not answer

- Which vendor, product, hospital, health system, regulator, or public program has the best workaround-monitoring process?
- Whether a local workaround log satisfies law, regulation, accreditation, certification, procurement, privacy, civil rights, labor, or professional-liability obligations.
- Whether a specific AI-enabled workflow is safe, unsafe, compliant, noncompliant, effective, fair, locally validated, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.
- Whether FDA device context applies to every healthcare AI workflow.
- Whether a specific workaround should be allowed, prohibited, disciplined, accepted, escalated, or used in clinical care.

## Phrasing discipline

Use public-facing terms such as "the source basis", "available governance sources", "workaround log", "bypass", "duplicate work", "non-use", "workflow fit", "fallback route", "escalation route", "review trigger", and "evidence gap".

Avoid language that points to non-reader operational details. The reader should see a healthcare AI governance article.

For `Selected Source Basis`, list public source families by name. Acceptable examples include NIST AI RMF, ONC SAFER guides, ONC decision-support intervention material, WHO, AMA, Joint Commission/CHAI, and bounded FDA AI/ML device context.
