# W26 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W26-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W26
Prepared: 2026-05-22

## Intended angle

This source set supports a cautious weekly report on health care AI infrastructure. The strongest through-line is not "AI has transformed clinical care." The stronger, source-grounded angle is that regulatory, health IT, and clinical-governance infrastructure is maturing while clinician adoption and training needs continue to rise.

## Source classes included

- FDA device and device-software oversight sources.
- FDA predetermined change control plan materials for AI-enabled device software.
- HHS and ASTP/ONC AI governance and health IT transparency sources.
- AMA physician sentiment/adoption survey data.
- HIPAA and WHO governance references for health-data boundary context.

## Source classes not included

- No private vendor claims.
- No patient-level data.
- No internal operator/governance documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No source asserting a new clinical outcome result for W26.

## Claims that are safe to make

- AI-enabled medical device oversight is increasingly tied to lifecycle management, change control, and transparency.
- Certified health IT is a major channel for algorithm-transparency policy.
- Physicians report broad awareness/use of AI, but training and implementation confidence remain explicit needs.
- Health-data governance remains a limiting condition for AI use in care delivery.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI tools are broadly improving patient outcomes.
- That FDA-authorized AI devices are widely deployed in clinical practice.
- That HTI-1 transparency makes predictive algorithms clinically safe.
- That self-reported physician AI use equals organization-wide AI deployment.
- That de-identification or HIPAA compliance eliminates all AI privacy risk.

## Publication gate guidance

If the generated report depends on claims outside these boundaries, mark the report as not publishable until additional sources are staged. Evidence warnings should remain visible if the report discusses adoption, clinical outcomes, or privacy beyond what the sources directly support.
