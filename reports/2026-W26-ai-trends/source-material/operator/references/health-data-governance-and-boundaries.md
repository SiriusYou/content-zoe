## Facts and observations

- HIPAA applies to covered entities and business associates, not to every consumer AI tool or every data broker that might interact with health-adjacent information.
- HIPAA permits some uses and disclosures of protected health information for treatment, payment, and health care operations, but that permission is not a blanket endorsement of arbitrary AI reuse.
- HHS de-identification guidance distinguishes de-identified data from protected health information only when the HIPAA standard is met. AI training or evaluation claims should not imply that "de-identified" means risk-free unless the method and governance are stated.
- WHO's AI health regulation guidance emphasizes privacy, security, data integrity, transparency, risk management, and independent validation considerations for AI systems in health.
- WHO warns that health AI systems can interact with sensitive personal information, which requires robust legal and regulatory safeguards.

## W26 framing

- Reader-safe headline: AI adoption in health care is constrained not only by model performance, but by health-data rights, de-identification quality, and governance boundaries.
- Use this source as a caution layer. It should support evidence-limit paragraphs rather than a claim that any one U.S. rule fully resolves AI data governance.
- Evidence limit: these sources are strong for legal/governance boundaries. They are not evidence of current adoption levels or model performance.
