## Facts and observations

- HHS describes a department-wide AI posture through a OneHHS approach, with AI positioned across internal operations, research, public health, and health-sector innovation.
- HHS's strategy and compliance materials emphasize governance and public trust for federal AI use. This is relevant to health care AI because HHS is both a regulator and a major payer/operator across public health programs.
- The HTI-1 final rule establishes algorithm transparency requirements for AI and other predictive algorithms that are part of certified health IT. ASTP/ONC frames the rule as part of health IT certification, interoperability, and information-sharing policy.
- The HTI-1 page states that ONC-certified health IT supports care delivered by more than 96 percent of hospitals and 78 percent of office-based physicians. That makes certified health IT a major distribution layer for algorithm-transparency requirements.
- HealthIT.gov's decision-support intervention material describes predictive decision support interventions as models or algorithms that derive relationships from training data and produce a prediction, classification, recommendation, evaluation, or analysis.
- The DSI material requires source-attribute information for decision support interventions. This is governance infrastructure, not proof that a given model is clinically effective.

## W26 framing

- Reader-safe headline: AI governance is becoming embedded in health IT certification and interoperability policy, not only in standalone AI-product review.
- Connect FDA and HTI-1 carefully: FDA covers regulated devices and device software; ASTP/ONC covers certified health IT and decision-support transparency. They overlap in practical clinical environments but are not the same regime.
- Evidence limit: the sources support claims about regulatory and certification infrastructure. They do not prove that transparency information is always complete, easy for clinicians to use, or sufficient to determine clinical safety.
