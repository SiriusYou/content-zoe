# W26 source briefing: clinician adoption, confidence, and training needs

Job: 2026-W26-ai-trends
Prepared for: AI in healthcare - weekly
Retrieved: 2026-05-22

## Sources

- American Medical Association, "2026 Physician Survey on Augmented Intelligence" PDF:
  https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/physician-ai-sentiment-report.pdf/

## Facts and observations

- AMA's 2026 physician AI survey was fielded from January 15 to February 2, 2026, with 1,692 U.S. physicians surveyed across specialties, practice settings, and career stages.
- AMA reports that physician awareness or use of AI in practice rose to 81 percent in 2026. This is a self-reported survey result, not an audit of deployed systems.
- The AMA survey reports that 92 percent of physicians want more AI education and training.
- Among physicians who had received any AI training, only 11 percent reported receiving "a lot" of training from any source.
- The survey's methodology says adoption was measured through self-reported incorporation of AI use cases across administrative, clinical, operational, patient-facing, and training-related domains.
- The survey cautions that adoption reflects physician self-report and does not independently verify organizational deployment.

## W26 framing

- Reader-safe headline: physician exposure to AI is broadening, but confidence infrastructure still lags adoption.
- Avoid treating self-reported AI use as proof of safe or mature deployment. The evidence supports rising familiarity/use and a training gap.
- Tie this to the regulatory sources: clinicians are asking for education, clinical evidence, implementation guidance, and transparency at the same time regulators are pushing source attributes and lifecycle documentation.
