# W35 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W35-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W35
Prepared: 2026-05-25

## Intended angle

This source set supports a broad healthcare-AI weekly report for a content product. The strongest angle is assurance: healthcare AI is no longer only an adoption story, and the reader-facing question is whether organizations can produce credible evidence, reviewability, change control, vendor accountability, privacy discipline, and human oversight.

The report title should be a reader-facing topic title, not a job label. Do not include `2026-W35` or any other week key in the H1 title. Week identity belongs in job metadata and README, not in the article headline. Also avoid repeating the "Healthcare AI's Next Test Is ..." title frame; recent reports already used that template.

## Source classes included

- Health-system AI assurance, governance, safety-learning, and internal-accountability references.
- Source attributes, algorithm transparency, interoperability, TEFCA, and HHS AI strategy references for reviewability.
- FDA AI-enabled device software, predetermined change control, and lifecycle-management references.
- Third-party AI risk, vendor evidence, supply-chain transparency, cybersecurity, and operational-resilience references.
- Physician experience, prior authorization concern, privacy, de-identification, WHO, and OECD governance references.

## Source classes not included

- No private project documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No patient-level data.
- No private vendor diligence.
- No direct clinical outcome dataset.
- No comprehensive market, funding, litigation, or M&A map.

## Claims that are safe to make

- Healthcare AI assurance requires evidence review, monitoring, safety learning, and accountable ownership.
- Source attributes, algorithm transparency, and interoperability can make AI systems easier to review, while not proving performance.
- AI-enabled device software materials illustrate the importance of lifecycle control and bounded change, while not applying unchanged to every workflow AI tool.
- Third-party AI risk and cybersecurity controls are part of healthcare AI governance because vendor systems shape data flows, evidence access, contracts, and incident response.
- Physician exposure and prior-authorization concern support human-accountability and workflow-governance pressure.
- Privacy, de-identification, WHO, OECD, and NIST references set boundaries and vocabulary, not proof of local readiness.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI broadly improves patient outcomes or lowers costs.
- That guidance adoption proves safe implementation, compliance, fairness, or ROI.
- That source attributes or transparency fields prove model performance.
- That FDA device software discipline validates non-device workflow AI.
- That vendor evidence, contract language, or a named framework proves resilience.
- That physician use or concern proves audited deployment quality.

## Publication gate guidance

Keep evidence warnings visible. Reject or restage if the generated report overstates clinical outcomes, deployment maturity, regulatory validation, vendor performance, cybersecurity readiness, privacy compliance, or assurance maturity. Also reject or edit if the H1 title includes a week key such as `2026-W35`, or if it repeats the "Healthcare AI's Next Test Is ..." title template.
