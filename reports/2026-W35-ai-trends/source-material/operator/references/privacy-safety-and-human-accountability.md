## Facts and observations

- AMA materials support the claim that physicians are encountering AI and that human accountability, training, prior authorization, and workflow impact remain active governance concerns.
- HIPAA and de-identification materials define privacy boundaries and methods; they do not certify that an AI deployment is compliant.
- WHO and OECD materials support human-centered, accountable, transparent, and context-sensitive governance language.
- These references support a cautious report about privacy, safety, and professional responsibility.
- They do not prove AI has improved outcomes, reduced administrative burden, prevented denials, or achieved local compliance.

## W35 framing

- Reader-safe headline direction: AI assurance must keep human accountability and privacy boundaries visible.
- Connect physician experience to training, escalation, documentation, and policy discipline.
- Treat privacy and ethics frameworks as boundaries, not evidence that local deployment quality has been achieved.

## Evidence limits

- Professional sentiment, privacy guidance, and ethics frameworks are useful for governance framing.
- They are not clinical outcome studies, compliance audits, payer-behavior audits, or vendor-performance evidence.
