## Facts and observations

- Joint Commission and CHAI materials support a health-system governance frame: responsible AI adoption requires internal governance, not only product selection.
- CHAI assurance materials support review routines around usefulness, safety, fairness, transparency, monitoring, and organizational accountability.
- AHRQ safety materials support treating AI-related harms and near misses as patient-safety learning signals, not only as technical defects.
- These sources support a report about assurance infrastructure: committees, evidence review, monitoring, incident learning, and accountable ownership.
- They do not prove that any named health system has achieved mature AI governance, broad clinical benefit, or reliable local performance.

## W35 framing

- Reader-safe headline direction: healthcare AI is moving from adoption claims toward assurance work.
- Use "assurance" to connect governance, monitoring, safety learning, and evidence review.
- Do not treat guidance, standards, or frameworks as evidence that deployment quality is already solved.

## Evidence limits

- Guidance and assurance documents are governance references, not implementation audits.
- They support claims about what responsible adoption should require, not claims about measured patient outcomes or system-wide maturity.
