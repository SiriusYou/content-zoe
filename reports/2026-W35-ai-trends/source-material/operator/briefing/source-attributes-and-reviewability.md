## Facts and observations

- HTI-1 materials support an algorithm-transparency and decision-support reviewability frame.
- Decision Support Intervention materials emphasize source attributes and technical performance / quality information as review surfaces.
- TEFCA and interoperability materials support the idea that governance depends on data visibility and exchange context.
- HHS AI strategy materials support responsible AI implementation as an enterprise and ecosystem governance problem.
- These materials support claims that AI review needs inspectable data, attributes, and workflow context.
- They do not prove that transparency fields alone establish safety, fairness, clinical value, or local readiness.

## W35 framing

- Reader-safe headline direction: reviewability is becoming a core healthcare AI requirement.
- Connect source attributes, algorithm transparency, interoperability, and HHS strategy to the ability to inspect AI systems.
- Avoid implying that documentation or exchange infrastructure is equivalent to validated performance.

## Evidence limits

- These sources describe control surfaces and policy direction.
- They do not independently validate a specific AI model, vendor, care pathway, or outcome claim.
