# Receiving user and workflow transfer

## Why the receiving user matters

The person who receives an AI-enabled output may not be the person who saw the original data, configured the workflow, reviewed the tool, or understood the evidence limits. That receiving user needs enough context to know what the output is meant to support and what it does not support.

A handoff note should keep the receiving user in view. The next action may depend on role, setting, timing, professional responsibility, available fallback, and whether the output is informational, advisory, triage-related, operational, or part of a documentation flow.

## Transfer situations the article may discuss

- shift change;
- team handoff;
- referral or escalation;
- transfer between clinical and operational teams;
- output moving from dashboard to task list;
- summary moving into documentation;
- alert moving to follow-up queue;
- decision-support output influencing a downstream action.

These are workflow examples, not universal clinical instructions. The article should not imply that every setting has the same handoff design or that any local action is legally required by the source basis.

## Important boundary

Do not present handoff context as a guarantee that the receiving user will act correctly. The source-bounded claim is narrower: context can make the output easier to interpret, question, route, and escalate when appropriate.
