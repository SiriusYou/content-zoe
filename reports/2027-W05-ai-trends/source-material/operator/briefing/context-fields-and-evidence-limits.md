# Context fields and evidence limits

## What has to travel with the output

The output value alone is rarely enough. A useful handoff note can carry the output purpose, reviewed use boundary, intended receiver, owner group, source attributes, evidence gaps, monitoring responsibility, fallback contact, escalation route, and next action when those fields exist.

The article may explain that these fields help separate what the output says from what the organization has actually reviewed. That distinction matters because a receiving user may otherwise treat an output as broader, newer, more certain, or more actionable than the source basis supports.

## Evidence-limit questions

- What is the output meant to support?
- Who is the intended receiver?
- What use boundary was reviewed?
- What source attributes matter?
- What evidence gaps remain unresolved?
- Who monitors concerns?
- Who can answer questions?
- What fallback or escalation path exists?

These questions can make handoff context more actionable. They do not prove that the workflow is safe, effective, fair, compliant, or mature.

## Source boundary

Available governance sources can support questions about documentation, visibility, workflow context, monitoring, contingency planning, and responsible adoption. They do not provide local handoff records, measured outcomes, legal conclusions, or named implementation proof.
