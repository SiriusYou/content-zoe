# Source attributes and local context

## Why context depends on the workflow

The usefulness of a handoff note depends on the workflow setting. A note that fits one AI-enabled function may not fit another. The intended receiver, output purpose, downstream action, source attributes, available fallback, owner group, monitoring responsibility, and escalation route can all affect what context has to travel.

The article may say that available governance sources support disciplined questions about documentation, workflow context, visibility, monitoring, contingency planning, and escalation. It must also say that those sources do not prove any local handoff record is complete, correct, safe, compliant, effective, fair, mature, or outcome-improving.

## Local context that may change

- clinical or operational setting;
- intended receiver;
- output purpose;
- downstream action influenced by the output;
- source attributes available to reviewers;
- evidence gaps;
- owner group;
- monitoring responsibility;
- fallback contact;
- escalation route.

The article should use these examples to explain why handoff notes can be useful, not to imply that every local record contains these fields.

## Public language guard

When describing evidence limits, use reader-facing terms such as "available governance sources", "the source basis", "local evidence", and "review record". Do not point readers toward non-reader operational details.

When naming the source basis for readers, use public source-family names. Evidence-limit language should focus on what the source basis can and cannot prove.
