# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Evidence posture

The source basis supports a cautious, governance-oriented article about handoff context for healthcare AI outputs. It can explain why documentation, workflow fit, decision-support visibility, human oversight, monitoring, escalation, contingency planning, responsible adoption, and bounded device context make output transfer risky when context is missing.

The source basis does not prove that any named organization has a handoff program. It does not supply local handoff logs, shift-change practices, owner rosters, committee charters, audit trails, utilization data, model-performance measurements, safety outcomes, equity outcomes, legal conclusions, or named implementation proof.

The article should therefore frame handoff notes as a routing and context discipline, not as proof that an AI-enabled workflow is safe, compliant, effective, fair, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.

## Required warning posture

Use explicit evidence-limit warnings in both locales. The warnings should say that the article is limited to source-bounded governance, health IT, decision-support, workflow documentation, safety-management, responsible-adoption, and bounded device-context material. They should also state that the article does not include local handoff logs, committee charters, audit logs, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.

Warning language must be locale-pure. English comments should contain English only. Chinese comments should contain Chinese only.

Warnings should not describe how evidence was prepared. Keep the warning focused on what the source basis can and cannot prove.

## Reader questions the article may answer

- Why does an AI-enabled output need context when it moves between people, teams, shifts, or settings?
- What can go wrong if a receiving user sees only the output value or label?
- What fields make handoff context operationally useful?
- How do use boundary, owner, fallback contact, escalation route, and evidence gaps fit together?
- Why should unknown, stale, or missing handoff context remain visible?
- What can the available governance sources support, and what can they not prove?

## Reader questions the article must not answer

- Which vendor, product, hospital, health system, regulator, or public program has the best handoff process?
- Whether a local handoff note satisfies law, regulation, accreditation, certification, procurement, privacy, civil rights, informed-consent, or professional-liability obligations.
- Whether a specific AI-enabled workflow is safe, compliant, effective, fair, locally validated, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.
- Whether FDA device context applies to every healthcare AI workflow.
- Whether a specific AI-enabled output should be accepted, escalated, ignored, or used in clinical care.

## Phrasing discipline

Use public-facing terms such as "the source basis", "available governance sources", "handoff note", "receiving user", "use boundary", "output purpose", "fallback contact", "escalation route", "review trigger", and "evidence gap".

Avoid language that points to non-reader operational details. The reader should see a healthcare AI governance article.

For `Selected Source Basis`, list public source families by name. Acceptable examples include NIST AI RMF, ONC SAFER guides, ONC decision-support intervention material, WHO, AMA, Joint Commission/CHAI, and bounded FDA AI/ML device context.
