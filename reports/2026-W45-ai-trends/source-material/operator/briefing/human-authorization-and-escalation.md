# Human authorization and escalation

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep "human authorization" concrete. The report should argue that a human approval step matters only when the person has enough context, time, authority, and escalation support to make a real decision. A final click should not be treated as proof of meaningful oversight.

## Source basis

- AMA augmented-intelligence materials emphasize AI as assistive and connect responsible use with physician workflow, patient transparency, training needs, privacy, cybersecurity, and professional responsibility.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports human autonomy, oversight, accountability, and protection of affected people.
- NIST AI RMF supports assigning roles, responsibilities, and risk-management activities across lifecycle contexts.
- FDA CDS materials support attention to whether a recommendation's basis is available for professional review.

## Reader-safe claims

- Human authorization should define who reviews, what they can see, what they can change, what they can reject, and what happens when they disagree with the AI output.
- Escalation should be available when the AI output is unclear, conflicts with local context, appears wrong, or falls outside intended use.
- A human review step can be weak if the interface hides the basis, makes override difficult, does not show uncertainty, or pressures the reviewer through defaults and queue design.
- Authorization duties should be matched to training, workload, and accountability; otherwise oversight can become symbolic.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim a human signature, click, or acceptance event proves meaningful review.
- Do not imply physicians or clinicians alone can absorb every AI authorization duty.
- Do not claim training ensures safe or compliant use.
- Do not imply every AI output requires the same type of human review.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support operational questions about authorization and escalation. They do not prove that any local human-review process is adequate, timely, unbiased, or safe.
