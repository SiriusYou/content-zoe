# Owner review and expiring decisions

## Owner confirmation

A carry-forward file should name accountable owners because open AI governance work needs follow-up. An owner may be a clinical, operational, technical, safety, privacy, security, analytics, revenue-cycle, informatics, or governance group depending on the workflow.

The report may say that a year-boundary review should confirm whether each owner still exists, still accepts responsibility, and still has a path to escalate concerns.

## Decisions that should not silently continue

The report may describe decisions that deserve explicit carry-forward treatment:

- pilot allowed to continue only under conditions;
- use boundary approved for one setting but not another;
- monitoring plan accepted with gaps;
- validation or local review deferred;
- change accepted temporarily;
- fallback path identified but not tested;
- retired or replaced item still leaving operational dependencies;
- owner transition pending.

Avoid saying that any of these conditions are illegal, noncompliant, or unsafe. The source-bounded claim is that these conditions are review-relevant and should remain visible.

## Escalation and decision rights

An open item is more useful when the file names who can resolve it or where it should go next. This may include a governance board, clinical leader, operational owner, health IT lead, safety office, privacy/security partner, vendor manager, or other accountable group.

Decision rights should connect to actions: continue, narrow, pause, retire, replace, reopen review, request evidence, validate locally, update training, update monitoring, or clarify use boundary.

## Boundaries

Do not invent local owners, due dates, committee structures, or authority maps. The report may list fields a local file should capture, but it cannot supply local answers.
