# FDA AI medical-device oversight signals

Source URLs:

- FDA AI-enabled medical devices list: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices
- FDA AI in Software as a Medical Device: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/artificial-intelligence-software-medical-device
- FDA digital health guidance list: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/guidances-digital-health-content

Grounded facts:

- FDA frames AI-enabled medical devices as an innovation area that still has to satisfy safety and effectiveness expectations. The FDA AI-enabled device list says listed devices met applicable premarket requirements, including review of safety/effectiveness and study appropriateness for intended use and technical characteristics.
- The FDA AI-enabled device list is transparency-oriented, not comprehensive. It identifies devices primarily through AI-related terms in authorization summaries and/or device classification, and FDA says the list will be updated periodically.
- FDA is considering more explicit identification of foundation-model or LLM-based functionality in future updates. The page says sponsors are encouraged to include enough information in public summaries to help identify AI-enabled and LLM-based functionality.
- The AI-enabled device list showed recent decision examples in late 2025, including radiology-heavy entries such as TruSPECT Processing Station, AIR Recon DL, and ART-Plan+.
- FDA's AI in SaMD page emphasizes lifecycle management. It says AI/ML medical-device development, deployment, use, and maintenance involve complex/dynamic processes that benefit from careful lifecycle management.
- FDA says AI/ML-enabled medical devices may need premarket review when modifications are significant or patient-risk-bearing.
- FDA's digital health guidance list showed a January 29, 2026 final Clinical Decision Support Software guidance, a January 6, 2026 final General Wellness guidance, an August 18, 2025 final Predetermined Change Control Plan guidance for AI-enabled device software functions, and a June 27, 2025 final Cybersecurity in Medical Devices guidance.

Interpretive signal for the report:

- The near-term trend is not simply "more healthcare AI." It is more healthcare AI under sharper documentation, lifecycle-management, cybersecurity, and transparency expectations.
- A report should avoid claiming that all healthcare AI is FDA regulated. The FDA sources are specific to medical devices, software as a medical device, and device software functions.
- A report may state that reader-facing governance needs to distinguish medical-device AI from administrative or content operations AI, because the regulatory evidence here is strongest for medical-device contexts.

Evidence limitations:

- FDA list data is not a complete census of all deployed AI tools.
- The source set does not include paid market databases, hospital procurement data, vendor deployment counts, or peer-reviewed clinical-performance summaries.
- Current evidence supports regulatory/governance trend claims, not market-share or revenue claims.
