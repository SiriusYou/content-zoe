# HHS AI strategy and governance signals

Source URLs:

- HHS Artificial Intelligence at HHS: https://www.hhs.gov/programs/topic-sites/ai/index.html
- HHS Artificial Intelligence Strategy & Implementation: https://www.hhs.gov/programs/topic-sites/ai/strategy-implementation/index.html
- 2025 HHS AI Strategic Plan PDF: https://www.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025%20HHS%20AI%20Strategic%20Plan_Full_508.pdf

Grounded facts:

- HHS describes a OneHHS approach to AI across its divisions, with a stated goal of keeping pace with evolving technologies while supporting practical and measurable impact across HHS and the healthcare sector.
- HHS's public AI page points readers to a use-case inventory of non-classified, non-sensitive current and planned AI use cases.
- HHS's strategy and implementation page says the department launched an initiative to make AI available to the federal workforce and integrate it across internal operations, research, and public health.
- HHS's strategy page separates an AI Strategy from a Compliance Plan; the page describes the compliance plan as focused on AI innovation, AI governance, and public trust in federal AI use.
- The existence of both strategy and compliance artifacts is a governance signal: AI adoption is being framed as both operational enablement and control/trust work.

Interpretive signal for the report:

- HHS's public posture supports a report theme that healthcare AI governance is moving from abstract principle to operating model: inventories, compliance plans, workforce access, and division alignment.
- The topic should avoid presenting HHS AI strategy as a direct mandate for private-sector health systems. The source supports federal/HHS governance posture and broader sector relevance, not a binding private-sector rule.
- The source set supports claims about governance emphasis: use-case visibility, public trust, federal workforce enablement, and cross-division alignment.

Evidence limitations:

- The staged HHS sources do not provide detailed implementation metrics, procurement totals, or outcome measurements.
- The sources support directional governance analysis, not quantified performance claims.
- If the generated report makes claims about private-sector adoption, it must mark them as beyond this source set unless tied to another staged source.
