# Ownership, risk tier, and use boundary

## Editorial use

Use this file to make the inventory operational rather than archival. The report should say that listing a workflow is not enough unless the inventory also names who owns it, what use is approved, and what risk questions determine review depth.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports governance, roles, responsibilities, mapping, measuring, managing, and risk prioritization.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance supports governance structures, local validation, and monitoring in organizational context.
- AMA augmented-intelligence material supports workflow, accountability, training, privacy, cybersecurity, and professional responsibility.

## Reader-safe claims

- Each inventory item should have an accountable owner or owner group.
- The record should state approved use, prohibited or unreviewed use, intended users, setting, patient or staff pathway, and escalation route.
- Risk tiering can help prioritize review, but a tier is a routing tool, not proof of safety or maturity.
- Review depth can depend on patient-facing impact, clinical decision influence, automation level, data sensitivity, user dependence, fallback availability, vendor dependency, and update cadence.
- Ownership should include who can continue, narrow, pause, retire, or reopen review for the item.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim risk tiering proves compliance or safety.
- Do not create a universal high/medium/low classification scheme as if it came from the staged sources.
- Do not claim ownership alone solves accountability.
- Do not invent local owners, decisions, or prohibited-use findings.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support ownership and prioritization questions. They do not include local risk classifications, governance committee minutes, authorization records, or legal review.
