# Embedded, indirect, and shadow AI

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep W52 from becoming a simple product list. The report should explain that healthcare AI can be embedded inside ordinary workflow software, analytics, documentation, operations, patient access, and decision-support layers.

## Source basis

- ONC Decision Support Interventions material supports visibility into logic, source attributes, references, and implementation information for health IT decision support.
- ONC SAFER materials support review of health IT systems, interfaces, contingency planning, and safe operations.
- WHO and AMA sources support oversight, accountability, workflow fit, and professional responsibility around health AI.

## Reader-safe claims

- Inventory should look beyond standalone AI products.
- Relevant AI-enabled workflows may sit inside EHR modules, clinical decision support, patient portals, ambient documentation, imaging support, coding and billing, scheduling, triage, referral routing, prior authorization support, operations queues, population-health analytics, or quality reporting.
- Embedded tools can be missed if governance only asks procurement for a list of AI vendors.
- Shadow or informal use should be surfaced for review rather than hidden until a problem occurs.
- The inventory should distinguish direct patient-care decision support from administrative, operational, research, documentation, and communication workflows.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim any specific hidden AI tool exists locally.
- Do not accuse users or teams of unauthorized AI use.
- Do not claim all embedded automation is AI.
- Do not claim every administrative AI workflow carries the same patient-safety risk.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support embedded-use discovery questions. They do not include local software scans, procurement records, user interviews, shadow-use reports, or implementation evidence.
