# Local AI inventory and status register

## Editorial use

Use this file to make W52 about a local AI inventory. The report should argue that governance cannot reliably review, update, monitor, retire, or explain AI-enabled workflows that the organization has not listed in a reviewable register.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF and AI RMF Core support inventory, governance, context mapping, roles, measurement, management, and lifecycle review.
- ONC SAFER System Management supports attention to health IT configuration, maintenance, validation, system inventory-like control, software, hardware, and interfaces.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance supports organizational governance, local validation, monitoring, and responsible adoption.

## Reader-safe claims

- The inventory should name the workflow, use case, owner, user group, setting, status, patient or staff pathway, and review date.
- Status should be operational, not decorative: proposed, piloted, active, narrowed, paused, retired, replaced, or pending review.
- The inventory should link each item to approved use, source material, evidence, monitoring, update/change records, near-miss records, renewal review, and retirement or fallback path when those records exist.
- A registry helps governance ask what exists before asking whether it is governed well.
- Unknown or incomplete fields should remain visible rather than being filled with assumptions.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim a local inventory proves maturity or safety.
- Do not claim every organization needs the same inventory schema.
- Do not invent item counts, risk tiers, owners, or status values as local facts.
- Do not claim any named organization has a complete or incomplete inventory.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support inventory and register questions. They do not include local inventory records, owners, item counts, status histories, procurement logs, or implementation proof.
