# Inventory hygiene, review, and retirement

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the inventory alive. The report should argue that a register becomes stale unless it has review cadence, update triggers, retired-state handling, and a way to reconcile with change records, near misses, renewal, and exit planning.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management, monitoring, measurement, management, and governance.
- ONC SAFER System Management and Contingency Planning support maintenance, system change, downtime, recovery, and safe health IT operations.
- WHO, AMA, and Joint Commission/CHAI sources support ongoing oversight, accountability, local validation, and responsible use.

## Reader-safe claims

- Inventory hygiene should include periodic review, update triggers, owner confirmation, retired-state records, and reconciliation with changes, near misses, incidents, and renewal decisions.
- A tool removed from active use can still need a record if it leaves data, workflow dependencies, contractual obligations, patient communication issues, or fallback questions behind.
- Inventory review should ask whether the item is still active, still in scope, still owned, still monitored, and still tied to current evidence.
- The register should not be treated as complete merely because it exists.
- Inventory review can surface duplicates, abandoned pilots, hidden dependencies, and workflows that changed without an updated record.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim inventory review proves governance maturity.
- Do not claim retired items have no remaining obligations or risk.
- Do not invent abandoned pilots, duplicates, or stale tools.
- Do not give contract, privacy, security, or records-retention legal advice.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support inventory hygiene and lifecycle questions. They do not include local retention policies, contract terms, data-deletion records, retirement plans, or operational results.
