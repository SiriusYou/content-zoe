# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this reference to keep the W52 report cautious. The report should offer a governance frame: "You cannot govern healthcare AI you cannot find." It must not offer a factual weekly market roundup, vendor ranking, product review, legal/regulatory opinion, maturity score, or proof that any local AI inventory is complete.

## What the sources support

- AI-enabled healthcare workflows can be reviewed through inventory, context mapping, ownership, use boundaries, source attributes, decision-support role, data dependencies, status, review cadence, monitoring, change history, near-miss links, fallback paths, renewal status, and retirement state.
- NIST AI RMF and AI RMF Core can support inventory and mapping questions. ONC SAFER and DSI materials can support health IT system management, decision-support visibility, and source-attribute questions.
- FDA AI/ML-enabled medical device materials can inform bounded questions about identifiable AI-enabled device functions. They must not be treated as a universal healthcare AI inventory.
- WHO, AMA, and Joint Commission/CHAI materials support oversight, accountability, local validation, responsible adoption, workflow fit, and professional responsibility.

## What the sources do not support

- No claim that any specific AI workflow, product, vendor, hospital, health system, or public program has a complete or incomplete AI inventory, is mature or immature, compliant or noncompliant, safe or unsafe, effective or ineffective.
- No local inventories, hidden-use audits, software scans, procurement logs, vendor lists, utilization data, user interviews, risk scores, maturity scores, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.
- No legal advice about FDA, ONC certification, HIPAA, procurement, civil rights, malpractice, cybersecurity, disclosure, data retention, records management, or professional responsibility.
- No claim that an inventory, dashboard, model card, procurement list, risk tier, governance board, source-attribute list, or review cadence proves safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, patient trust, outcome improvement, or operational maturity.

## Required evidence warnings

The final report should preserve evidence-grade warnings in each locale, but each report file should use only its own language. Warning count may vary if every warning is source-bounded and locale-pure:

1. A leading warning that the draft is limited to staged AI inventory, health IT safety, decision-support, risk-management, governance, device-context, and professional-source material and does not include local inventories, hidden-use audits, procurement records, utilization logs, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.
2. A mid-report warning near the discussion of inventory fields, dashboards, risk tiers, ownership, source attributes, and review cadence noting that these practices can make AI use more reviewable but do not prove completeness, safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, patient trust, outcome improvement, or operational maturity.

Locale rule:

- `report.en.md` must include only English `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments.
- `report.zh.md` must include only Chinese `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments.
- Do not duplicate both languages into both report files.

Suggested EN warning:

`EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN: This draft is limited to staged AI inventory, health IT safety, decision-support, risk-management, governance, device-context, and professional-source material. It does not include local inventories, hidden-use audits, procurement records, utilization logs, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.`

## Reader promise

The report may argue that healthcare AI governance needs a local inventory before it can review, monitor, update, or retire AI-enabled workflows. It must not claim that the sector has solved AI inventory, shadow AI discovery, maturity measurement, risk classification, or embedded AI oversight.
