# Research Brief: Local Healthcare AI Inventory

## Objective

Produce a source-bounded report arguing that healthcare AI governance needs a local, reviewable inventory of AI-enabled workflows before it can review, monitor, update, or retire them.

## Working Thesis

Use this frame: "You cannot govern healthcare AI you cannot find."

Prefer a title close to:

- You Cannot Govern Healthcare AI You Cannot Find
- Healthcare AI Inventory Has to Be Local
- Before Healthcare AI Governance, Make the List

Do not put the week key in the report H1.

## What The Report Should Do

- Treat the report as an AI inventory and use-register argument, not a market roundup.
- Explain that governance starts with knowing what AI-enabled workflows exist locally.
- Keep the focus on a reviewable register: what is in use, where it is embedded, who owns it, what pathway it touches, what status it has, what evidence applies, and when it should be revisited.
- Show that embedded and indirect AI may appear inside ordinary workflow systems, not only in standalone AI products.
- Make the inventory operational: ownership, approved-use boundary, risk-routing questions, monitoring, review cadence, and retirement state.

## Core Claims Supported By The Staged Sources

- A local inventory should capture workflow, use case, owner, user group, setting, patient or staff pathway, status, and review date.
- Status should be operational rather than decorative, for example: proposed, piloted, active, narrowed, paused, retired, replaced, or pending review.
- Useful linked fields include approved use, source material, evidence, monitoring, change records, near-miss records, renewal review, fallback path, and retirement state when those records exist.
- Inventory scope should include embedded and indirect AI in EHR modules, decision support, documentation, patient portals, imaging support, coding and billing, scheduling, triage, referral routing, prior authorization support, operations queues, analytics, and quality reporting.
- Each item should have an accountable owner or owner group, an approved-use boundary, intended users, setting, escalation route, and review depth tied to impact questions.
- Source attributes, data dependencies, output type, integration point, human action expected, monitoring signal, and fallback path strengthen the register when available.
- Inventory hygiene matters: periodic review, update triggers, owner confirmation, retired-state handling, and reconciliation with changes, near misses, incidents, renewal, and exit planning.

## Non-Negotiable Evidence Boundaries

- Do not claim any named vendor, product, hospital, health system, or public program has a complete or incomplete AI inventory.
- Do not claim any specific hidden or shadow AI tool exists locally.
- Do not invent local inventory counts, owners, status histories, risk scores, utilization data, procurement facts, or implementation proof.
- Do not present inventories, dashboards, source attributes, governance boards, or risk tiers as proof of safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, patient trust, outcome improvement, or operational maturity.
- Do not give legal or regulatory advice.
- Do not let FDA device-context material stand in for all healthcare AI.

## Recommended Report Structure

1. Open with the local-inventory thesis and a leading evidence warning.
2. Explain why governance questions fail when the underlying AI workflow list is missing.
3. Define what a usable local register should contain.
4. Expand the scope beyond standalone tools into embedded and indirect AI workflows.
5. Explain ownership, use boundaries, and risk-based review routing.
6. Show how source attributes, data links, and workflow context improve reviewability without proving safety.
7. Close on lifecycle hygiene: review cadence, updates, retirement state, and lingering obligations after a tool leaves active use.
8. Include a short `Selected Source Basis` section in each locale.

## Selected Source Basis To Emphasize

- NIST AI RMF and AI RMF Core for inventory, context mapping, governance, lifecycle review, and risk prioritization.
- ONC SAFER materials for system management, maintenance, contingency, and health IT operational review.
- ONC Decision Support Interventions material for source attributes, references, logic visibility, and implementation information in decision support.
- WHO, AMA, and Joint Commission/CHAI material for oversight, accountability, workflow fit, local validation, and responsible adoption.
- FDA AI/ML-enabled medical device material only as bounded device-context evidence for some identifiable AI-enabled functions.

## Source Gaps To State Transparently

- No local inventory records are present.
- No hidden-use audit, software scan, procurement log, vendor list, utilization log, user interview, or measured outcome data is present.
- No legal analysis or named implementation proof is present.
- The report can recommend review questions and inventory fields, but it cannot prove completeness or local adoption facts.
