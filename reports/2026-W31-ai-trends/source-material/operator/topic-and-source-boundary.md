# W31 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W31-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W31
Prepared: 2026-05-24

## Intended angle

This source set supports a broad healthcare-AI weekly report for a content product. The strongest angle is evidence discipline after adoption: physician use is broad, federal infrastructure work is making AI more operationally relevant, and the next credibility test is whether organizations can evaluate tools, govern vendors, and separate regulated-device evidence from ordinary workflow claims.

## Source classes included

- Physician adoption, training, and augmented-intelligence framing from AMA.
- HHS / ONC interoperability, TEFCA, and AI-enabled health technology coordination.
- FDA AI-enabled device software, real-world performance monitoring, and digital-health guidance.
- Health-sector third-party AI risk, supply-chain transparency, and cybersecurity governance.
- International responsible-scaling and health-AI regulatory considerations from OECD and WHO.
- Privacy, de-identification, and general AI risk-management boundaries from HHS and NIST.

## Source classes not included

- No internal operator/governance documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No patient-level data.
- No private financial diligence.
- No vendor product performance dossier.
- No comprehensive funding, M&A, or market-share map.

## Claims that are safe to make

- Healthcare AI is now operational enough that governance and evaluation quality matter more than novelty claims.
- Physician use and institutional experimentation create pressure for training, workflow fit, and human accountability.
- Interoperability and data-liquidity work can make AI more deployable, but infrastructure progress is not clinical validation.
- FDA-regulated AI-enabled device software should be distinguished from non-device workflow, administrative, and consumer-facing AI.
- Third-party AI risk is a procurement, cybersecurity, privacy, accountability, and monitoring issue.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI has broadly improved patient outcomes.
- That all physician-reported use equals audited production deployment.
- That interoperability progress proves AI tools are safe.
- That FDA policy materials validate non-device workflow AI.
- That vendor claims or de-identification labels are sufficient evidence of safety, compliance, or performance.
- That responsible scaling readiness is uniform across countries, institutions, or use cases.

## Publication gate guidance

If the generated report overstates patient outcomes, universal deployment, regulatory maturity, vendor validation, or country-level readiness, reject or restage with additional sources. Evidence warnings should remain visible where survey, policy, infrastructure, cybersecurity, or governance materials cannot support stronger claims.
