## Facts and observations

- OECD frames responsible scaling of AI in health as a policy-readiness and safeguards problem.
- WHO's health-AI regulatory materials emphasize privacy, security, transparency, documentation, data quality, lifecycle tracking, and risk management.
- HIPAA privacy obligations remain central for covered entities and business associates, but HIPAA does not cover every health-adjacent AI, vendor, consumer, or data-broker context.
- HHS de-identification guidance shows why claims about de-identified data should be tied to method and governance rather than the label alone.
- NIST's AI Risk Management Framework provides general vocabulary for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks; it is not health-specific validation evidence.

## W31 framing

- Reader-safe headline: international and privacy/risk sources all point toward evidence discipline, transparency, and controls rather than unqualified acceleration.
- Use OECD and WHO for responsible-scaling and regulatory-consideration framing.
- Use HIPAA, de-identification, and NIST as boundary-setting references, not as proof that a specific AI product is safe or compliant.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support reader cautions and governance framing.
- They do not establish deployment scale, clinical effectiveness, product performance, or legal compliance for any specific organization or vendor.
