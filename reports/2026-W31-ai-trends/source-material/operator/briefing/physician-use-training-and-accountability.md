## Facts and observations

- AMA reports that more than 80 percent of physicians use AI professionally in 2026.
- AMA frames "augmented intelligence" as AI designed to support physicians and care teams rather than replace professional judgment.
- AMA materials identify training needs, workflow concerns, patient use of AI, and potential skill-loss questions as part of the adoption environment.
- The physician survey supports claims about exposure, professional use, and perceived needs; it does not establish clinical outcome improvement or independently audited deployment quality.
- The adoption signal is most useful when tied to accountability: as tools enter physician workflows, organizations need education, review, escalation, and human oversight practices.

## W31 framing

- Reader-safe headline: physician use is now broad enough that the question is not whether healthcare will encounter AI, but whether organizations can train and govern use responsibly.
- Treat self-reported physician use as adoption pressure, not proof of safety or effectiveness.
- Use AMA's augmented-intelligence framing to keep physician accountability and workflow fit central.

## Evidence limits

- Survey evidence is self-reported and supports adoption/sentiment claims only.
- It does not prove that specific tools are clinically validated, outcome-improving, or uniformly safe across settings.
