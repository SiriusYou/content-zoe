## Facts and observations

- HSCC's third-party AI risk guide focuses on supply-chain transparency, discovery, disclosure, and procurement controls for AI used in healthcare.
- HSCC frames third-party AI as a cybersecurity and governance issue involving contract terms, accountability, performance expectations, data rights, and vendor dependence.
- FDA's medical-device cybersecurity guidance connects connected-device risk to design, labeling, premarket submission content, and quality-system considerations.
- Third-party AI risk can affect clinical workflows even when a tool is not a regulated medical device, because AI may be embedded in vendor systems that staff use every day.
- Vendor and cybersecurity guidance supports governance claims, not a claim that recommended controls are broadly implemented.

## W31 framing

- Reader-safe headline: the health AI risk story is increasingly about vendor accountability and procurement discipline.
- Separate connected-device cybersecurity from broader AI vendor risk, while noting that both sit inside operational resilience.
- Avoid implying that publication of guidance means organizations have fully implemented it.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support risk-management and procurement-control framing.
- They do not quantify implementation maturity or independently verify vendor performance.
