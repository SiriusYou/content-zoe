## Facts and observations

- FDA's AI-enabled device software draft guidance focuses on lifecycle management and marketing-submission recommendations for AI-enabled device software functions.
- FDA maintains materials on AI/ML-enabled medical devices and digital-health guidance, which helps distinguish regulated device software from broader non-device AI.
- FDA's public-comment request on real-world AI-enabled medical-device performance highlights a live evidence question: how performance should be measured and evaluated after deployment.
- These materials support a regulated-device evidence and monitoring frame, not a blanket safety claim about all healthcare AI tools.
- Real-world monitoring is especially important because model performance can depend on setting, population, workflow, data quality, and post-deployment change.

## W31 framing

- Reader-safe headline: the regulated-device side of healthcare AI is increasingly about lifecycle evidence, not just clearance or launch.
- Distinguish device software from ambient documentation, administrative tools, clinical workflow assistants, and consumer health AI.
- Treat FDA attention to real-world performance as a sign that post-deployment evidence remains an unresolved governance problem.

## Evidence limits

- FDA materials describe oversight direction, device categories, and evidence questions.
- They do not validate non-device workflow AI and do not prove that every cleared or marketed product performs equally well in every clinical setting.
