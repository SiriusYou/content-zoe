## Facts and observations

- HHS links health technology leadership to data liquidity, affordability, interoperability, and an AI-enabled health care system.
- HHS describes TEFCA as national interoperability infrastructure and reports large-scale records exchange through the network.
- HHS AI strategy materials frame governance, public trust, and responsible implementation as part of federal AI work.
- ONC/HealthIT.gov materials around HTI-1 connect health IT certification, algorithm transparency, and information sharing.
- These sources show infrastructure and governance direction. They do not prove that AI applications using the infrastructure are clinically safe, fair, or effective.

## W31 framing

- Reader-safe headline: federal infrastructure work is making AI deployment more plausible and more governable, but infrastructure progress is not the same as clinical validation.
- Treat interoperability and data-liquidity signals as enabling conditions, not outcome evidence.
- Connect algorithm transparency to governance discipline, not to a claim that algorithmic tools are mature everywhere.

## Evidence limits

- Federal infrastructure and certification materials support policy-direction and control-surface claims.
- They do not establish provider-level deployment quality, patient outcomes, or product performance.
