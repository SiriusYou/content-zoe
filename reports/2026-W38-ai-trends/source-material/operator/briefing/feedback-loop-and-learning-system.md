# Feedback loop and learning system

## Editorial use

Use this file to frame the report around learning loops. The safe thesis is that responsible healthcare AI is becoming less about a one-time launch decision and more about whether the organization can learn from use, incidents, overrides, complaints, and model changes after deployment.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AHRQ patient-safety and learning-health-system materials support feedback loops, event review, near-miss learning, and improvement routines.
- NIST AI RMF supports ongoing Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage work rather than one-time risk documentation.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports lifecycle assurance, monitoring, and evidence review as recurring responsibilities.
- Joint Commission responsible data and AI governance materials support accountable oversight and safety-oriented data practices.

## Supported claims

- A healthcare AI deployment is more credible when the organization can show how it learns from live use.
- Useful learning inputs include clinician overrides, incident reports, patient complaints, workflow friction, documentation gaps, data drift, and vendor change notices.
- The learning loop should connect observations to decisions: refine workflow, retrain staff, change thresholds, limit use, demand vendor evidence, pause the tool, or retire it.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim that learning loops prove safety, better outcomes, or lower cost.
- Do not imply that every organization already has mature AI learning infrastructure.
- Do not rank health systems or vendors.

## Evidence warning

The report can say that learning loops are an emerging governance requirement. It cannot say they prove real-world effectiveness without local data.
