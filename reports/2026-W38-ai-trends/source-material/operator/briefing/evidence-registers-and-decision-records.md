# Evidence registers and decision records

## Editorial use

Use this file to argue that healthcare AI governance needs durable records. A responsible organization should be able to reconstruct why a tool was approved, what evidence was reviewed, what limits were accepted, and what changed after deployment.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- ONC/HealthIT decision-support transparency concepts support source attributes, intended-use context, and user-facing information about recommendations.
- NIST AI RMF supports risk documentation, accountability, and traceability across risk-management functions.
- CHAI assurance guidance supports evidence collection around intended use, data, performance, monitoring, and accountability.
- FDA lifecycle materials for regulated AI-enabled device software illustrate the value of documented modifications and postmarket monitoring in bounded regulated contexts.

## Supported claims

- Evidence registers can make governance inspectable by recording source evidence, known limitations, approval rationale, review dates, monitoring duties, and change history.
- Decision records reduce memory loss when staff change or when an AI tool is modified.
- The value of a register is not bureaucracy; it is the ability to revisit assumptions when local behavior changes.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not treat documentation as proof of quality.
- Do not imply that all healthcare AI requires FDA-style documentation.
- Do not claim a tool is compliant because it has a register.

## Evidence warning

Evidence registers improve reviewability. They are not themselves proof that an AI system is safe, effective, fair, or locally appropriate.
