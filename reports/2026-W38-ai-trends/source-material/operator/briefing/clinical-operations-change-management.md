# Clinical operations and change management

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the report grounded in operations. Healthcare AI changes clinical work even when it is not making autonomous decisions. A credible deployment plan should define workflow changes, training, escalation, downtime behavior, and who owns updates.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AMA augmented-intelligence principles and physician AI adoption materials support training, transparency, physician responsibility, and careful integration into clinical practice.
- WHO and OECD governance principles support human oversight, accountability, transparency, and context-sensitive deployment.
- HHS AI strategy and health-system governance materials support responsible public-sector and health-sector use of AI, but should be treated as directional guidance, not deployment proof.
- AHRQ and patient-safety concepts support the idea that operational change should include feedback and safety learning.

## Supported claims

- AI governance is partly change management: training, role clarity, workflow design, escalation, and documentation practices determine whether a tool can be used responsibly.
- Human oversight only matters when the workflow gives people time, authority, and information to act.
- Change management should include a plan for what happens when the system is wrong, unavailable, updated, or challenged by a user.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not say human oversight eliminates risk.
- Do not imply clinician adoption proves tool quality.
- Do not claim the staged sources show successful implementation.

## Evidence warning

The report can say that change management is necessary for responsible use. It cannot claim that a specific deployment is safe or accepted in practice without local evidence.
