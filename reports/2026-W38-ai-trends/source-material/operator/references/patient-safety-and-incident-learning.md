# Patient safety and incident learning

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect AI governance to established safety practice. The report should argue that healthcare AI needs incident-learning capacity: not just predeployment risk review, but a way to notice, investigate, learn, and respond when AI-mediated work creates harm or near misses.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- AHRQ patient-safety resources support incident review, near-miss reporting, and learning routines.
- Joint Commission responsible AI/data governance materials support oversight and accountability in health-system settings.
- HHS 405(d) / HICP and HSCC materials support resilience, incident response, third-party risk, and health-sector operational preparedness.
- NIST AI RMF supports risk management as an ongoing lifecycle activity.

## Supported claims

- Safety learning should include AI-related incidents and near misses, including wrong recommendations, inappropriate automation reliance, alert fatigue, workflow mismatch, privacy concerns, and vendor-related disruptions.
- Incident learning needs a taxonomy, a reporting route, and a response owner.
- AI incidents can involve clinical, operational, privacy, security, and vendor dimensions at once.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a specific incident occurred.
- Do not imply the staged sources measure incident frequency.
- Do not suggest that reporting systems alone prevent harm.

## Evidence warning

The report can say that incident learning is an important missing operating capability. It cannot quantify incident risk from the staged source set.
