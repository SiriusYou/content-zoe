# Topic and source boundary

## Intended reader-facing angle

The report should focus on this thesis:

Healthcare AI needs a learning loop. Governance is becoming more credible when organizations can preserve decision records, monitor data and workflow changes, learn from incidents, and update operating controls after deployment. The practical question is not only whether AI can be introduced, but whether the organization can keep learning from it after it enters care delivery.

## Required style

- Do not include any week key in the H1 title.
- Use a title frame that differs from recent frames: "Next Test Is ...", "Assurance Phase", "Show Its Work", and "Operating Question".
- Keep the report source-bounded and evidence-cautious.
- Preserve explicit `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in both locales.
- If using a leading doc-level warning before the H1, keep it concise and ensure the H1 itself remains clean and reader-facing.
- Avoid promotional language and avoid ranking vendors or health systems.

## Safe claims

- Responsible healthcare AI is becoming a learning-loop problem after deployment.
- Evidence registers, decision records, drift review, incident learning, and change management are connected operating duties.
- Frameworks and guidance can improve governance questions, but they do not prove local deployment quality.
- Human oversight requires workflow design, documentation, and escalation authority.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim clinical outcomes, cost savings, staffing improvements, compliance, fairness, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, or vendor resilience are proven.
- Do not state that an incident occurred unless a staged source specifically says so.
- Do not treat documentation, transparency labels, or model cards as proof of implementation.
- Do not cite runtime metadata, transcripts, run-state files, or repo-internal files as reader sources.

## Publication gate

Reject or restage if the draft:

- uses only job metadata or generated context as evidence;
- includes any week key in the H1;
- repeats the recent title templates;
- cites runtime metadata;
- overstates frameworks into outcome proof;
- omits the evidence-limits posture;
- treats a learning-loop requirement as proof that the sector already has mature learning loops.
