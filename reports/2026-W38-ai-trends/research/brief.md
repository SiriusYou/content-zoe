# Research Brief: Healthcare AI Learning Loops

## Core Thesis

The report should argue that healthcare AI governance becomes more credible after deployment when an organization can keep learning from live use. The strongest source-bounded angle is that evidence registers, decision records, drift review, incident learning, and clinical change management are connected operating duties rather than separate compliance tasks.

## Drafting Priorities

1. Lead with the shift from one-time approval to ongoing learning.
2. Show how the operating pieces connect: preserve decision records, monitor data and workflow changes, capture incidents and overrides, and update controls when assumptions break.
3. Keep the human-oversight point concrete: oversight only matters when workflow design, escalation authority, training, downtime handling, and update ownership are defined.
4. Close by stating that frameworks improve the questions organizations ask, but they do not prove local quality, safety, fairness, or effectiveness.

## Supported Claims To Use

- Responsible healthcare AI is increasingly a post-deployment learning-loop problem.
- Evidence registers and decision records make governance reviewable by preserving rationale, limits, review dates, monitoring duties, and change history.
- Drift review is a governance task because changes in patient mix, coding, documentation, vendor updates, or care pathways can make the original evidence base less reliable for local use.
- Incident learning should cover wrong recommendations, automation overreliance, alert fatigue, workflow mismatch, privacy concerns, and vendor-related disruptions.
- Change management is part of governance because responsible use depends on training, role clarity, escalation paths, downtime behavior, and update ownership.

## Claims To Avoid

- Do not claim outcomes, cost savings, staffing improvements, compliance, fairness, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, or vendor resilience are proven.
- Do not say an incident occurred or quantify incident frequency.
- Do not present registers, transparency labels, model cards, or learning loops as proof of quality or implementation success.
- Do not imply the sector already has mature learning-loop infrastructure.

## Recommended Structure

1. Opening: the real governance question starts after the tool enters care delivery.
2. Middle section: connect records, drift review, incident learning, and workflow change into one operating loop.
3. Final section: emphasize evidence limits and the absence of staged local deployment proof.

## Style And Publication Constraints

- Keep the eventual report source-bounded and explicitly evidence-cautious.
- Preserve `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in both `en` and `zh`.
- Do not put the week key in the eventual report H1.
- Avoid the recent title frames `"Next Test Is ..."`, `"Assurance Phase"`, `"Show Its Work"`, and `"Operating Question"`.
- Avoid promotional language and do not rank vendors or health systems.

## Source Gaps

The staged material supports a governance argument, not a performance claim. There is no staged local evidence showing successful implementation, measured drift, clinical outcomes, cost impact, or incident rates for a named deployment.
