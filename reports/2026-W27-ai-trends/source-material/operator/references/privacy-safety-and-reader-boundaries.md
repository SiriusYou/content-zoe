## Facts and observations

- HIPAA establishes privacy obligations for covered entities and business associates, but it does not cover every health-adjacent consumer AI tool or data broker.
- HHS de-identification guidance matters because AI-related claims about de-identified data should be tied to method and governance, not just the label.
- WHO's AI health regulatory guidance emphasizes privacy, security, transparency, data quality, risk management, and independent validation.
- NIST's AI Risk Management Framework provides cross-sector language for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks. It is useful as a general risk-management reference but is not a health-specific adoption metric.

## W27 framing

- Reader-safe headline: privacy and safety guardrails remain part of the AI adoption story, especially where health data or clinical workflows are involved.
- Do not imply HIPAA or any single framework fully solves health AI governance.
- Evidence limit: these sources support caution and governance framing, not deployment scale or clinical outcome claims.
