# Monitoring, fallback, and escalation responsibility

## Monitoring responsibility

A useful owner record should say who reviews monitoring signals and who responds when those signals require action. Monitoring responsibility may include reviewing thresholds, exceptions, complaints, user concerns, near misses, incidents, or performance signals when those records exist.

The report should not invent local monitoring results or claim that monitoring exists where no record is provided.

## Fallback responsibility

Owner records should also connect to fallback and contingency questions. If an AI-enabled workflow is unavailable, narrowed, paused, retired, or replaced, someone should know who switches to fallback, how output is reconciled, and where unresolved fallback questions are escalated.

## Escalation route

Escalation is needed when an owner cannot resolve an issue alone. The route may involve clinical leadership, operations, health IT, safety, privacy, security, legal, compliance, vendor management, or governance groups depending on local structure.

Do not invent a local structure. The report may name the kinds of groups that often need to be consulted, not the structure of any named organization.

## Boundary

Monitoring, fallback, and escalation responsibility improve reviewability. They do not prove resilience, safety, compliance, or operational maturity.
