# Source attributes and local context

## Why context matters

The usefulness of a disagreement path depends on the workflow setting. A path that fits one AI-enabled function may not fit another. The intended users, output use, downstream decision, local fallback options, owner group, monitoring responsibility, and escalation route can all affect what a practical challenge route looks like.

The article may say that available governance sources support disciplined questions about contestability, documentation, and escalation. It must also say that those sources do not prove any local challenge route is complete, correct, safe, compliant, effective, fair, mature, or outcome-improving.

## Local context that may change

- clinical or operational setting;
- intended users;
- output use;
- downstream decision influenced by the output;
- alternate action available to the user;
- documentation location;
- monitoring responsibility;
- owner group;
- escalation contact;
- review trigger.

The article should use these examples to explain why disagreement paths can be useful, not to imply that every local record contains these fields.

## Public language guard

When describing evidence limits, use reader-facing terms such as "available governance sources", "the source basis", "local evidence", and "review record". Do not point readers toward non-reader operational details.

When naming the source basis for readers, use public source-family names. Evidence-limit language should focus on what the source basis can and cannot prove.
