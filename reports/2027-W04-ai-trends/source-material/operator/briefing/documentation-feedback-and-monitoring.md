# Documentation, feedback, and monitoring

## Why disagreement should become visible signal

If users repeatedly disagree with an output, cannot act on it, or work around it, that pattern may be important governance information. A disagreement path can help convert isolated concerns into visible signals for monitoring and review.

The article may connect documentation to lifecycle governance. A challenge route can create a way to notice friction, evidence gaps, output misfit, unclear ownership, or a need to review the use boundary.

## Documentation questions

- Where is disagreement recorded?
- Who can see the concern?
- Who reviews repeated concerns?
- When does a concern become a monitoring signal?
- When does it trigger escalation or workflow review?
- How is the concern distinguished from proof of harm?

These questions can make disagreement more actionable. They do not prove that the workflow is safe, effective, fair, compliant, or mature.

## Monitoring boundary

Monitoring concern counts, override patterns, and user feedback can support review questions when those data exist. They should not be invented. The article should not claim measured rates, patterns, outcomes, or incidents.
