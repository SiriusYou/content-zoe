# Escalation and review triggers

## Escalation route

A disagreement path is weaker if users do not know where unresolved concerns go. Escalation routes are useful because they connect output-level disagreement to a person or group able to review the concern, clarify the use boundary, request evidence, coordinate fallback, or route governance review.

The article may describe escalation as a routing function. It must not imply that escalation alone resolves the issue or satisfies a legal, clinical, operational, or accreditation requirement.

## Review trigger categories

- repeated disagreement with similar outputs;
- concern about missing context;
- concern about changed setting or users;
- concern about the output influencing a different decision;
- workflow workaround or bypass becoming common;
- unclear owner or escalation contact;
- monitoring signal that no one is reviewing;
- disputed use boundary;
- evidence gap reaching a decision point.

These triggers do not prove harm, noncompliance, or model failure. They show why a concern may deserve review.

## Keeping unresolved status visible

Unknown, unusable, or disputed disagreement routes should remain visible. If no one can tell how a user challenges an output, who reviews concerns, or where repeated concerns go, the item is not resolved. The next action may be owner confirmation, route clarification, documentation design, or escalation.
