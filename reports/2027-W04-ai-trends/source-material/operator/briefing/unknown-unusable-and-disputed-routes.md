# Unknown, unusable, and disputed routes

## Unknown route

An unknown disagreement route should remain visible. If users cannot tell where to document a concern, who sees it, or who can respond, the workflow has not made challenge actionable. The next action may be route clarification, owner confirmation, documentation design, or escalation.

The article should frame unknown route status as a governance routing problem, not as proof that the workflow is unsafe or noncompliant.

## Unusable route

A route can exist on paper but still be hard to use. It may be too slow, disconnected from the workflow, unclear about who responds, or unavailable to the people who need it. The source-bounded claim is not that any specific route is unusable. The claim is that usability matters if the organization expects users to raise concerns.

## Disputed route

Disagreement routes can be disputed when multiple groups control different parts of a workflow or when the output affects decisions outside the original use boundary. A record should show the dispute and route it to a decision-maker rather than hiding it behind a generic escalation label.

The article may connect this point to owner-of-record and review-date thinking from prior themes, but it should keep the primary focus on disagreement paths and contestability.
