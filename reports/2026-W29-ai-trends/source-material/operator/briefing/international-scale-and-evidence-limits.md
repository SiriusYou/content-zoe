## Facts and observations

- OECD says AI's potential in health is not fully realized because of fragmented data foundations, non-aligned policies and practices, and structural and governance barriers to scalability.
- OECD reports that while AI is used broadly in administration across OECD member countries, national-level scale-up remains limited in examples such as medical imaging.
- OECD identifies risks including skewed data, privacy and security risks, insufficient transparency or oversight, job displacement, and depersonalization.
- OECD's policy checklist emphasizes guardrails, enabling practices, capacity and capability, oversight, measurement, monitoring, and public engagement.
- OECD reports that only a minority of OECD countries have specific AI-in-health strategies, oversight bodies, regulatory sandboxes, AI-aware health technology assessment approaches, AI-aware procurement guidelines, workforce AI approaches, or AI-in-health legislation.

## W29 framing

- Reader-safe headline: the policy problem is no longer whether AI matters in health; it is how to scale responsibly when data, oversight, procurement, workforce capacity, and public trust are uneven.
- Use OECD as a broad policy-readiness reference, not as a U.S.-specific adoption metric.
- Treat "scale" as a governance challenge, not as proof of universal operational maturity.

## Evidence limits

- OECD data supports cross-country policy readiness and governance claims.
- It does not validate a particular vendor, product, health system, or patient outcome.
