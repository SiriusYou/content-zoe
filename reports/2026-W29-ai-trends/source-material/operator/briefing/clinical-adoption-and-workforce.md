## Facts and observations

- AMA reports that the share of physicians using AI in their practices reached 81 percent in 2026, more than double the 38 percent reported in 2023.
- AMA reports that the average number of AI use cases per physician increased from 1.1 in 2023 to 2.3 in 2026.
- AMA identifies medical research summarization and clinical-care documentation as common physician uses.
- The AMA survey is self-reported physician sentiment/adoption evidence. It supports a practitioner-exposure and workflow-use story, not audited deployment, clinical effectiveness, or patient-outcome claims.
- The AMA evidence supports a training and governance angle: adoption is broadening, but responsible use still depends on education, workflow fit, and guardrails.

## W29 framing

- Reader-safe headline: physician exposure to AI is now mainstream enough that governance and training are practical operating issues, not speculative future concerns.
- Avoid treating physician self-reporting as audited production deployment.
- Avoid treating adoption as proof of improved outcomes.

## Evidence limits

- The survey supports adoption and perception claims only.
- It does not establish whether AI tools are safe, effective, equitable, or independently validated in each clinical setting.
