## Facts and observations

- FDA's AI software-as-medical-device page places AI-enabled device software within a sequence of lifecycle, predetermined-change-control, transparency, and cross-center AI policy work.
- FDA's January 2025 draft guidance on AI-enabled device software functions is described as proposing lifecycle considerations and marketing-submission recommendations.
- FDA's February 2026 medical-device cybersecurity guidance provides recommendations for cybersecurity device design, labeling, and premarket submission documentation for devices with cybersecurity risk.
- HHS says ONC will work with OCIO, HHS operating divisions, providers, health IT developers, and patients to advance health technology policies and regulations for a safe, secure, interoperable future state, including coordination for AI in clinical care.
- HHS's AI strategy material frames AI governance and public trust as part of federal use and sector-facing AI work.

## W29 framing

- Reader-safe headline: federal sources point toward lifecycle oversight, cybersecurity resilience, interoperability, and cross-agency coordination rather than a single AI rule solving healthcare adoption.
- Distinguish regulated AI-enabled medical-device software from certified health IT and from general workflow or administrative AI.
- Treat cybersecurity as part of the adoption story when AI is embedded in connected medical devices or software-dependent workflows.

## Evidence limits

- Policy and guidance sources show oversight direction and expected controls.
- They do not prove that deployed tools are clinically effective or that every healthcare organization has mature governance.
