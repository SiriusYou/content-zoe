## Facts and observations

- HSCC's third-party AI risk guide focuses on AI supply-chain transparency and the risks healthcare organizations face when relying on third-party AI tools and vendors.
- Reported recommended practices include comprehensive AI governance policies, use-case justification, and contract language addressing data ownership, AI training, and performance standards.
- The HSCC preview framed health-sector AI cybersecurity guidance around governance, third-party risk, education, enablement, and shared responsibility with vendors.
- Third-party AI risk is not only a technical cybersecurity issue. It also affects procurement, contracting, data rights, oversight, and accountability.
- The TechTarget article is secondary reporting; use it only to interpret or contextualize the HSCC guide, not as the sole basis for claims.

## W29 framing

- Reader-safe headline: health-system AI governance is moving from abstract principles toward vendor-risk, procurement, and contract controls.
- Emphasize that third-party AI can expand an organization's dependency surface even when the AI tool is not a regulated medical device.
- Avoid claiming that any specific organization has solved third-party AI risk.

## Evidence limits

- The sources support governance and risk-management framing.
- They do not quantify adoption scale or prove that recommended controls are widely implemented.
