## Facts and observations

- HIPAA establishes privacy obligations for covered entities and business associates, but it does not cover every health-adjacent consumer AI tool, vendor workflow, or data broker.
- HHS de-identification guidance matters because AI-related claims about de-identified data should be tied to method and governance rather than to the label alone.
- WHO's AI health regulatory guidance emphasizes privacy, security, transparency, data quality, risk management, and independent validation.
- NIST's AI Risk Management Framework provides cross-sector language for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks. It is useful as a general risk-management reference but is not a health-specific deployment metric.

## W29 framing

- Reader-safe headline: privacy, safety, and risk management remain part of the adoption story, especially when AI touches clinical workflows or health data.
- Do not imply that HIPAA or any single framework fully solves health AI governance.
- Do not treat de-identification as a magic word; tie claims to method, context, and controls.

## Evidence limits

- These sources support reader cautions and governance framing.
- They do not establish deployment scale, clinical effectiveness, or product performance.
