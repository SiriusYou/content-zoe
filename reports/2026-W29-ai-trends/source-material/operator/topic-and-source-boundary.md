# W29 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W29-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W29
Prepared: 2026-05-23

## Intended angle

This source set supports a broad healthcare-AI weekly report for a content product. The strongest angle is operational maturity with guardrails: physician use is becoming common, federal and international sources are formalizing governance expectations, and safety/security sources keep the adoption story bounded.

## Source classes included

- Physician adoption and training-demand evidence from AMA.
- FDA AI-enabled device software and medical-device cybersecurity guidance.
- HHS / ONC health technology and AI governance signals.
- OECD cross-country policy readiness evidence for scaling AI in health.
- Health-sector third-party AI and supply-chain risk guidance.
- Privacy, safety, and risk-management references for reader boundaries.

## Source classes not included

- No internal operator/governance documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No patient-level data.
- No private financial diligence.
- No claim that any product improves patient outcomes.
- No comprehensive vendor or market map.

## Claims that are safe to make

- Healthcare AI adoption is increasingly visible in physician work and health-system operations.
- Federal policy sources are emphasizing safe, secure, interoperable, and coordinated AI use.
- FDA materials distinguish regulated medical-device AI from broader workflow or administrative AI.
- Governance, security, transparency, workforce capacity, and procurement controls remain central adoption constraints.
- Third-party AI risk is a real governance and cybersecurity issue for healthcare organizations.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI has broadly improved patient outcomes.
- That every health system is deploying AI at scale.
- That vendor product claims are independently verified.
- That FDA authorization or policy transparency equals clinical safety.
- That HIPAA compliance alone resolves all AI data-governance risks.
- That cross-country policy readiness is uniform.

## Publication gate guidance

If the generated report overstates clinical outcomes, universal deployment, product effectiveness, or regulatory maturity, reject or restage with additional sources. Evidence warnings should remain visible where survey, policy, cybersecurity, or governance sources cannot support stronger claims.
