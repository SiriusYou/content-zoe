# Review rights and local ownership

## Editorial use

Use this file to tie intake to accountable ownership. The report should argue that a front-door process is incomplete unless it identifies who can approve, limit, monitor, question, and revisit the AI use case after intake.

## Source basis

- WHO health AI governance guidance emphasizes accountability and responsiveness to healthcare workers and affected communities.
- AMA augmented-intelligence materials emphasize AI's assistive role, physician and patient transparency, and the need for governance across device and non-device AI.
- Joint Commission and CHAI responsible-use guidance emphasizes policies, local validation, monitoring, and use that organizations can adapt to context.
- NIST AI RMF supports assigning risk-management responsibilities across design, development, deployment, use, and evaluation.

## Reader-safe claims

- Intake should name the local owner, clinical or operational sponsor, affected user group, and route for escalation.
- Review rights matter because AI questions are often cross-functional: clinical leadership, IT, compliance, privacy, security, procurement, data governance, quality, and patient-safety teams may all hold part of the answer.
- Intake should state who can say no, narrow the intended use, require additional evidence, pause a pilot, or revisit an approved use when conditions change.
- Ownership should survive handoff from procurement or pilot launch into production monitoring.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim multidisciplinary review guarantees better outcomes.
- Do not imply physician oversight alone resolves all governance risks.
- Do not claim a particular governance committee structure is required by every source.
- Do not turn local ownership into proof of accountability in practice.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support ownership and review-right questions. They do not prove that any local governance body can execute those responsibilities well.
