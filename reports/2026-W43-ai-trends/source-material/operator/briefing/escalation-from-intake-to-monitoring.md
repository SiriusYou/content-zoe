# Escalation from intake to monitoring

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect W43 to the previous beats without repeating them. The report should say intake is the front door that makes later transparency, evidence, monitoring, learning, and exit decisions possible.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF organizes risk work across governance, mapping, measurement, and management.
- FDA predetermined-change-control-plan principles for machine-learning-enabled medical devices emphasize bounded changes and monitoring for deployed models in device contexts.
- ONC DSI materials link source attributes with intervention risk-management practices for predictive DSIs in covered certified-health-IT contexts.
- Joint Commission and CHAI responsible-use guidance mentions policies, local validation, monitoring, and use as adaptable governance practices.
- HSCC third-party AI guidance addresses monitoring, vendor performance evaluation, traceability, disclosure, and escalation for high-risk products.

## Reader-safe claims

- Intake should define the next checkpoint: what must be reviewed before use, what must be monitored after use, and what should trigger escalation.
- A stronger intake record can include expected user behavior, known limits, validation needs, monitoring signals, change-notification requirements, downtime or fallback expectations, and reconsideration triggers.
- Intake gives monitoring a baseline: what was the tool approved to do, under what assumptions, and with what owner.
- Intake also gives exit planning a starting point: if the tool changes, underperforms, loses vendor support, or no longer fits workflow, the organization knows which use case is affected.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim monitoring prevents harm.
- Do not imply predetermined change control applies outside the relevant medical-device context.
- Do not turn an escalation trigger into evidence that escalation works.
- Do not repeat W41's exit-plan thesis as the main story; use exit only as one downstream dependency.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support intake as a connector between classification and downstream governance. They do not prove local monitoring quality, incident response, or retirement discipline.
