# Intake register and use-case classification

## Editorial use

Use this file to make intake concrete. The report should frame intake as a first operating layer: a way to identify AI-enabled use cases before they disappear into procurement, EHR configuration, vendor updates, departmental pilots, or individual workflow habits.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF 1.0 and AI RMF program materials support lifecycle risk management for AI products, services, and systems.
- NIST's AI RMF program describes the framework as intended to improve the ability to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into design, development, use, and evaluation.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports accountability to health workers and affected communities across design, deployment, and use.
- Joint Commission and CHAI describe responsible AI adoption guidance as internal governance that can be adapted to organizations at different stages of AI maturity.

## Reader-safe claims

- A useful intake record can start with plain questions: what is the tool, who will use it, what workflow it touches, what decision or task it influences, what data it uses, who developed or supplies it, and who owns local oversight.
- Intake is not the same as approval. It is the routing step that determines which evidence, review, monitoring, and owner are needed.
- AI intake should include both visible products and embedded capabilities, including vendor-supplied features, locally built models, analytics tools, documentation tools, patient-facing assistants, and administrative automation.
- Intake can keep governance from depending on informal knowledge held by one department or vendor manager.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim an intake register is complete unless the source set includes an audited inventory.
- Do not imply intake alone creates ethical, safe, fair, compliant, or trustworthy AI.
- Do not claim a universal intake form is enough for every clinical, operational, research, and administrative AI use.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support intake as a governance requirement. They do not prove any organization has achieved complete discovery or strong implementation.
