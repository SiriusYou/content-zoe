# Regulatory routing: device, certified health IT, and non-device AI

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the report precise about routing. The safe point is that healthcare AI tools need different review paths depending on what they are and how they are used. The report should not collapse medical-device software, certified health IT decision support, administrative AI, and general workflow tools into one category.

## Source basis

- FDA's AI-enabled medical-device materials describe authorized AI-enabled medical devices, software as a medical device, and lifecycle considerations for AI-enabled device software.
- FDA states that its AI-enabled medical-device list supports transparency, but also says public summaries are not all inclusive and the list is not comprehensive.
- FDA AI/ML software-as-medical-device materials say FDA reviews medical devices through appropriate premarket pathways and that AI/ML device modifications may need review depending on significance or risk.
- ONC HTI-1 and the Decision Support Interventions test method describe source attributes and intervention risk-management practices for predictive DSIs supplied by certified health IT developers as part of a Health IT Module.
- ONC materials note that NIST AI RMF concepts may be used to facilitate intervention risk management, while no particular framework is required for that certification criterion.

## Reader-safe claims

- Intake should ask what category a tool falls into before assigning a review path.
- A regulated AI-enabled medical device, a certified-health-IT predictive DSI, an ambient documentation feature, a scheduling assistant, and a locally built analytics model may require different evidence and governance questions.
- Public device-list or certification information can help route review, but it is not a complete evidence file.
- Intake should record uncertainty when the regulatory category is unclear, then route the case to qualified review rather than smoothing over the ambiguity.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not imply every healthcare AI tool is FDA-regulated.
- Do not imply every decision-support tool is an ONC-covered predictive DSI.
- Do not treat FDA authorization, public summaries, source attributes, or certification criteria as proof of local fit or local performance.
- Do not provide legal advice about regulatory status.

## Evidence limit

The sources support a routing frame: classification affects which questions must be asked. They do not prove safety, effectiveness, compliance, or local deployment quality.
