# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this reference to keep the W43 report cautious. The report should offer a governance frame: "Healthcare AI needs an intake front door." It must not offer a factual weekly market roundup, vendor ranking, case-specific performance claim, or regulatory opinion.

## What the sources support

- AI intake and classification can make governance more operational.
- Different healthcare AI tools need different routing questions depending on intended use, users, data, vendor role, workflow impact, and regulatory context.
- Public device lists, source attributes, certified-health-IT criteria, and vendor disclosures can help classification, but they are incomplete as evidence of local performance.
- Local ownership, review rights, and escalation paths are necessary if intake is meant to affect real workflows.
- Third-party and embedded AI deserve discovery because AI features can enter healthcare through procurement, vendor updates, and user behavior.

## What the sources do not support

- No claim that intake proves safety, effectiveness, fairness, cybersecurity readiness, privacy safety, compliance, clinical benefit, or patient trust.
- No claim that every healthcare AI tool is regulated, certified, high-risk, or clinically consequential.
- No claim that any specific hospital, vendor, or product has succeeded or failed.
- No claim that an intake registry, checklist, model card, contract, or source-attribute record proves local implementation quality.
- No legal advice about FDA, ONC, HIPAA, malpractice, procurement, or contract obligations.

## Required evidence warnings

The final report should preserve two evidence-grade warnings:

1. A leading warning that the draft is limited to staged governance, regulatory-routing, and third-party-risk source material and does not include local inventory evidence, deployment evidence, outcome data, or named case proof.
2. A mid-report warning near the discussion of intake records, device lists, source attributes, and vendor disclosures noting that those artifacts can route review but do not prove local safety, effectiveness, compliance, or operational maturity.

Suggested EN warning:

`EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN: This draft is limited to staged governance, regulatory-routing, and third-party-risk source material. It does not include local inventory evidence, deployment evidence, outcome data, or named case proof.`

Suggested ZH warning:

`EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN: 本草稿仅限于已暂存的治理、监管路由和第三方风险资料。不包含本地清单证据、部署证据、结果数据或具名案例证明。`

Suggested mid-report EN warning:

`EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN: Intake records, device-list entries, source attributes, and vendor disclosures can route review, but they do not prove local safety, effectiveness, compliance, or operational maturity.`

Suggested mid-report ZH warning:

`EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN: 接入记录、设备清单条目、来源属性和供应商披露可以帮助路由审查，但不能证明本地安全性、有效性、合规性或运营成熟度。`

## Reader promise

The report may argue that intake is the front door for accountable healthcare AI governance. It must not claim that the sector has solved discovery, review, or routing.
