# User notice and training

## Editorial use

Use this file to make the change record useful to people in the workflow. The report should argue that update governance fails if the only people who know about the change are technical owners.

## Source basis

- AMA augmented-intelligence material supports attention to workflow, training, accountability, privacy, cybersecurity, professional responsibility, and the patient-physician relationship.
- WHO health-AI ethics material supports transparency, human oversight, accountability, and protection of affected people.
- ONC SAFER materials support safe use of health IT by clinicians and staff.

## Reader-safe claims

- Users should know when an update changes what the tool shows, how it should be interpreted, when it should be overridden, when fallback should be used, and who owns questions.
- Notice should be specific enough for the workflow, not a generic announcement that "AI was updated."
- Training may need to change when an update changes output meaning, user roles, documentation expectations, escalation paths, or patient-facing communication.
- Patients or caregivers may need clearer routes to ask questions or correct information if an updated workflow changes their experience.
- The record should say who was notified and what support remains unresolved.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim notice proves transparency or informed use.
- Do not give legal advice about consent, disclosure, medical-device labeling, malpractice, or professional liability.
- Do not invent user notices, training attendance, patient communications, or complaint patterns.
- Do not imply every update requires patient-facing notice.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support notice and training questions. They do not include local communications, training records, consent analysis, patient-facing notices, or legal review.
