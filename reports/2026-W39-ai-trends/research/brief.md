# Research Brief

## Thesis

Healthcare AI trust is shifting to the data boundary. The core reporting question is not only whether an AI system performs in a test setting, but whether an organization can explain data origin and limits, constrain reuse and vendor access, surface source attributes in decision context, and provide patients and clinicians with usable routes for questions or challenge.

## Draft Angle

- Emphasize governance duties that sit closest to data handling and AI-mediated workflow decisions.
- Connect five duties into one operating frame: source attributes, patient disclosure, secondary-use controls, vendor constraints, and feedback or recourse.
- Treat privacy, security, and trust as reasons for governance discipline, not as proven outcomes.

## Recommended Structure

1. Open with the shift from model-centric trust claims to data-boundary accountability.
2. Show why source attributes matter only when tied to intended use, limits, workflow risk, and review responsibility.
3. Extend the argument to patient-facing disclosure: explain what AI is doing, what it is not doing, and who remains accountable.
4. Add secondary-use governance and vendor access as the places where data stewardship becomes operational, contractual, and auditable.
5. Close with feedback and recourse as the learning loop that keeps transparency from becoming one-way notice.

## Safe Claims To Carry Forward

- Responsible healthcare AI depends on data stewardship, not only model selection.
- Source attributes are useful when they help users inspect provenance, intended use, limitations, and escalation routes.
- De-identification is one control inside a broader governance system, not a complete trust strategy.
- Vendor controls matter when outside parties can influence data, models, updates, support, or analytics.
- Patient-facing trust depends partly on clear disclosure plus accountable review routes for questions and challenges.

## Claims To Avoid

- Do not claim compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, fairness, clinical safety, or patient trust.
- Do not equate disclosure with informed consent or patient understanding.
- Do not imply de-identification removes re-identification risk.
- Do not treat metadata, model cards, transparency labels, contracts, or governance language as proof that controls work in practice.
- Do not claim patients used, benefited from, or approved recourse or secondary data use.

## Evidence Posture

- Preserve explicit `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in both locales.
- Make clear that the staged material is governance guidance and editorial framing, not deployment evidence.
- State source gaps plainly: no staged evidence demonstrates real-world outcomes, patient comprehension, local implementation quality, legal compliance, or vendor performance.
