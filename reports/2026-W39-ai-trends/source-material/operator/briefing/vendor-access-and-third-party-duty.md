# Vendor access and third-party duty

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect AI trust with third-party controls. AI systems, analytics platforms, cloud services, and data partners can all touch sensitive workflows or data. The report should frame vendor governance as a patient-safety and trust issue without claiming a specific cybersecurity posture.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- HHS 405(d) Health Industry Cybersecurity Practices and HC3 materials support sector-wide attention to cybersecurity, third-party access, resilience, and patient-safety implications.
- Joint Commission Responsible Use of Health Data materials support data controls and limits on use when data is transferred or reused.
- NIST AI RMF supports assigning roles, documenting risks, monitoring deployed systems, and managing third-party and supply-chain implications where relevant.
- WHO ethics and governance guidance supports accountability across public and private actors involved in health AI.

## Supported claims

- Vendor access controls are part of AI governance when outside parties can influence models, data pipelines, system updates, support workflows, or analytics.
- Contracts and operating controls should address permitted use, audit rights, data retention, breach or incident reporting, model-change notice, and evidence obligations.
- Third-party risk can be clinical, operational, privacy, security, and reputational at the same time.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim a vendor or health system is secure.
- Do not rank vendors.
- Do not state that a breach, incident, or misuse occurred unless a staged source specifically says so.
- Do not treat contract language as proof that controls work in practice.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support vendor governance and access controls as necessary duties. They do not prove cybersecurity readiness, resilience, or vendor performance.
