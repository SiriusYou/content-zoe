# Secondary use and de-identification governance

## Editorial use

Use this file to show that data stewardship extends beyond direct care. Healthcare AI often depends on data reuse for quality improvement, operations, discovery, model development, or vendor work. The safe claim is that secondary use needs governance, limits, and transparency even when data is de-identified.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- Joint Commission Responsible Use of Health Data materials identify governance, de-identification, data controls, limitations on use, algorithm validation, and patient transparency as relevant areas for responsible secondary data use.
- HIPAA de-identification concepts provide a legal privacy frame, but do not by themselves answer every trust, governance, or re-identification concern.
- WHO ethics and governance guidance supports accountability, transparency, inclusiveness, and protection of people affected by health data use.
- NIST AI RMF supports documenting context and managing risks across the lifecycle.

## Supported claims

- De-identification should be treated as one control in a broader governance system, not as a complete trust strategy.
- Secondary-use governance should define who can access data, what uses are allowed, how re-identification risk is controlled, and how patients or communities are informed.
- AI development and product work can blur the line between internal improvement, vendor collaboration, and commercial reuse, making use limitations and auditability important.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim de-identification eliminates re-identification risk.
- Do not claim HIPAA compliance, Joint Commission certification, or any legal status for a particular organization.
- Do not imply patients have approved secondary use unless a staged source specifically says so.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support secondary-use governance as a trust requirement. They do not prove legal compliance, privacy safety, or patient acceptance.
