# Privacy, security, and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this file to constrain the report's claims. Privacy, security, and patient trust should be presented as reasons for governance discipline, not as outcomes already achieved. The report should be careful with legal and cybersecurity language.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- HIPAA de-identification concepts support discussion of privacy safeguards, but do not prove all data uses are safe or trusted.
- HHS 405(d), HICP, and HC3 materials support cybersecurity practices and sector coordination for healthcare and public health.
- WHO ethics and governance guidance supports human rights, accountability, transparency, and protection from harm.
- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management and documentation of risk decisions.

## Supported claims

- AI data governance sits at the intersection of privacy, cybersecurity, clinical operations, vendor management, and patient communication.
- Stronger trust requires both technical controls and social controls: access limits, use limits, auditability, transparency, and accountable review.
- Reader-facing claims should distinguish guidance, controls, and aspirations from measured deployment results.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim HIPAA compliance, privacy safety, cybersecurity readiness, fairness, clinical safety, or patient trust.
- Do not cite runtime metadata, `.runs`, transcripts, run-state files, DB rows, or repo-internal files as reader evidence.
- Do not use stale week keys, prior-week titles, or previous report prose as source material.
- Do not convert governance guidance into proof of outcomes.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources define boundaries for safe interpretation. They do not provide direct evidence of compliance, real-world performance, patient understanding, or operational maturity.
