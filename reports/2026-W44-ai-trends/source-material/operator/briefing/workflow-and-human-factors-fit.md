# Workflow and human-factors fit

## Editorial use

Use this file to make W44 about how AI fits into real work. The report should argue that an AI-enabled workflow is not ready simply because it has a model description, policy, intake record, or source attribute. Someone still has to ask whether the people expected to use or oversee it can do so under actual workflow conditions.

## Source basis

- FDA human-factors materials describe device-user systems as involving users, use environments, and user interfaces.
- FDA usability guidance for medical devices is a useful disciplined example for thinking about use-related hazards, intended users, intended uses, and use environments.
- NIST AI RMF supports mapping the context in which an AI system is used and managing risks across the lifecycle.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports attention to human autonomy, oversight, accountability, and context-sensitive use.

## Reader-safe claims

- Workflow-fit review should ask who sees the AI output, what other information they see at the same time, what task they are performing, what pressure they are under, and what authority they have to question or override the output.
- AI can change work even when it is framed as assistive: it can change documentation, routing, prioritization, communication, alerts, review queues, and handoffs.
- Usability and workflow questions should be revisited when the model, interface, staffing pattern, vendor release, or local protocol changes.
- Human-factors review is an evidence question, not a slogan. The record should show what was checked, by whom, with which user group and use environment in mind.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim a human-factors review proves the AI is safe or effective.
- Do not claim medical-device usability guidance governs every non-device AI workflow.
- Do not claim users will notice or correct AI errors unless the staged source supports that conclusion.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support human-factors and workflow-fit questions. They do not include local usability testing, time-motion study, observation, or user-performance evidence.
