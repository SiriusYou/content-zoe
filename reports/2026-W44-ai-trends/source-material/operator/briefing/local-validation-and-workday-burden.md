# Local validation and workday burden

## Editorial use

Use this file to connect W44 to local validation without repeating W40's evidence frame. The report should focus on workload and workday fit: whether the AI changes the amount, timing, difficulty, or accountability of work in ways the organization can observe.

## Source basis

- Joint Commission/CHAI guidance announcement references policies, local validation, monitoring, and use as responsible AI adoption topics.
- NIST AI RMF supports mapping context, measuring risks, and managing risks across the lifecycle.
- AHRQ patient-safety and alert-fatigue materials support attention to workflow, implementation, and human-technology interaction.
- AMA materials connect AI adoption to workflow, physician burden, responsibility, and training needs.

## Reader-safe claims

- Local validation should include workday effects, not only model-output quality.
- Workload evidence can include time to review, handoff complexity, duplicate documentation, review-queue volume, escalation friction, training completion, and user-reported burden.
- Burden reduction should be treated as a claim to test locally, not a default property of AI.
- If an AI tool creates new review obligations, organizations should decide who owns those tasks and what gets deprioritized.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim AI reduces burnout, documentation burden, cost, staffing pressure, or time burden without staged evidence.
- Do not claim workflow fit proves safety, fairness, privacy, security, compliance, or patient trust.
- Do not rank workflows, vendors, or health systems.
- Do not imply local validation has been completed by any named organization.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support workload and local-validation questions. They do not include local workday measurements, clinician-burden data, or implementation audits.
