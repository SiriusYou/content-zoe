# Device usability and non-device boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this file to avoid overgeneralizing FDA device materials. The report can use device human-factors guidance as a disciplined example of how usability, intended users, intended uses, and use environments matter, but it must not imply that every healthcare AI tool is a regulated medical device.

## Source basis

- FDA human-factors and usability engineering materials focus on medical devices and use-related hazards.
- FDA human-factors considerations describe the device-user system through users, use environments, and user interfaces.
- FDA AI-enabled medical-device materials can help route review where the tool is device software.
- ONC DSI materials are a separate certified-health-IT context with source attributes and intervention risk-management practices for covered predictive DSIs.

## Reader-safe claims

- The same human-factors questions can be useful across contexts, but the regulatory path differs by tool type and intended use.
- A regulated AI-enabled medical device, a certified-health-IT predictive DSI, an ambient documentation feature, a patient-facing assistant, and an administrative model should not be treated as one governance category.
- Device usability concepts can help readers understand why users, use environments, and interfaces matter.
- When the category is unclear, the safe governance move is qualified review rather than casual generalization.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not provide legal advice about regulatory status.
- Do not imply FDA human-factors guidance covers every healthcare AI workflow.
- Do not imply ONC predictive DSI criteria cover every decision-support use case.
- Do not treat device authorization, certification, source attributes, or usability documentation as proof of local workflow fit.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support category-aware routing and human-factors questions. They do not prove that any particular AI tool fits a device, certified-health-IT, or non-device category.
