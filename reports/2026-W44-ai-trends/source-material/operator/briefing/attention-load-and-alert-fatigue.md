# Attention load and alert fatigue

## Editorial use

Use this file to make the burden argument concrete. The report should not claim that AI creates or fixes alert fatigue in a measured way. It should say that healthcare has already learned that technology-mediated prompts can fail when they compete for scarce attention, and AI should be evaluated against that lesson.

## Source basis

- AHRQ PSNet's alert fatigue primer describes how clinician workflows can be affected by computerization and how alert fatigue arises from the interaction between technology and busy human beings.
- PSNet describes alert fatigue as a patient-safety concern and connects mitigation to human factors, implementation, leadership, and shared responsibility.
- ONC decision-support materials support the idea that decision support appears inside workflow and that source attributes/risk management matter for covered predictive DSIs.
- AMA augmented-intelligence materials keep AI framed as assistive and connected to physician workflow and responsibility.

## Reader-safe claims

- AI prompts, summaries, triage signals, documentation suggestions, and decision-support outputs all compete for attention.
- A workload-aware AI review should ask whether the output is interruptive, passive, mandatory, dismissible, time-sensitive, or easy to misunderstand.
- Useful review evidence can include override patterns, escalation rates, duplicate work, alert frequency, review-queue growth, complaint patterns, handoff friction, and user feedback.
- Alert fatigue is a cautionary analogy: a technically plausible prompt can still fail if it overloads users or appears at the wrong moment.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim AI has reduced or increased alert fatigue without local measurement.
- Do not imply every alert or prompt is harmful.
- Do not claim attention-load review prevents adverse events.
- Do not treat user feedback as the same thing as measured clinical safety.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support attention-load and alert-fatigue risk framing. They do not quantify W44 AI alert burden or prove any deployment outcome.
