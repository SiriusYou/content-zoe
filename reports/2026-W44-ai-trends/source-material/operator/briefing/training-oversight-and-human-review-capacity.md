# Training, oversight, and human-review capacity

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep "human in the loop" concrete. The report should argue that human review only matters when the human has enough context, time, training, authority, and escalation support to act.

## Source basis

- AMA augmented-intelligence materials emphasize AI as assistive and connect physician responsibility, workflow, risks, use cases, and training needs.
- WHO health AI governance guidance supports human oversight, accountability, and protection of people affected by AI.
- NIST AI RMF supports roles, responsibilities, and risk-management activities across AI lifecycle contexts.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible AI guidance announcement points to policies, local validation, monitoring, and use that organizations can adapt to context.

## Reader-safe claims

- Human review should be described operationally: who reviews, what they review, what context they receive, what they can change, what they can escalate, and what happens if they disagree with the AI output.
- Training should cover intended use, known limits, workflow steps, escalation routes, documentation expectations, and change notices.
- Oversight capacity is limited. Adding AI review tasks without time, staffing, or authority can turn "human oversight" into a paper control.
- Patient-facing or staff-facing AI uses may require different communication and training patterns.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim training ensures safe use.
- Do not imply physician accountability alone solves workflow, vendor, privacy, security, or operational risk.
- Do not claim any local team has sufficient oversight capacity without staged evidence.
- Do not imply every AI use requires the same level of training or review.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support concrete questions about training and human-review capacity. They do not prove that any local oversight process is adequate.
