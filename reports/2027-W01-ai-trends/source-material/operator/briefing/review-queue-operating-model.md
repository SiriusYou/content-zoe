# Review queue operating model

## Core idea

The first review cycle of a new year should treat AI governance as continuing work. The prior carry-forward file preserved unresolved questions; the review queue decides how those questions become review work.

The queue is not a proof artifact. It is an operating surface that helps route open items to owners, review triggers, evidence needs, monitoring review, validation, fallback review, retirement review, or escalation.

## Useful queue fields

The source-bounded report may describe fields such as:

- workflow or AI-enabled function;
- owner or owner group;
- queue trigger;
- current operational status;
- last review date or review thread;
- next action;
- evidence gap;
- monitoring gap;
- source-attribute gap;
- validation question;
- fallback or contingency question;
- near-miss, incident, or user-concern link;
- decision needed;
- escalation route.

## Queue status

Queue status should make work visible. Safe status examples include intake-needed, owner-confirm-needed, evidence-needed, monitoring-review-needed, validation-needed, fallback-review-needed, decision-needed, paused, retired, and closed.

The report should not invent local due dates or claim these labels are universal requirements.

## Boundary

A queue can improve review routing, but it does not prove that the organization has complete inventory, mature governance, compliant practice, or safe AI use.
