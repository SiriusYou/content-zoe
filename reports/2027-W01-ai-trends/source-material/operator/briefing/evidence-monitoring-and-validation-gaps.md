# Evidence, monitoring, and validation gaps

## Evidence gaps

A review queue should separate evidence that exists from evidence that is missing, stale, vendor-only, unreviewed, or not locally validated. This helps reviewers avoid mistaking a queue entry for proof.

The source basis supports source attributes, references, context, monitoring, measurement, documentation, and lifecycle review as governance concerns. It does not provide local validation results or measured outcomes.

## Monitoring gaps

Monitoring-related queue fields can preserve questions such as:

- what signal is monitored;
- who reviews the signal;
- when it was last reviewed;
- whether thresholds or escalation paths are defined;
- whether a monitoring exception exists;
- whether user feedback, complaints, near misses, or incidents need review.

## Validation gaps

Validation questions may arise when use changes, users change, workflow context changes, source attributes change, model behavior changes, downstream action changes, or a local review was deferred.

The report should treat validation as a review need, not as a claim that a workflow is invalid or unsafe.

## Boundary

Evidence, monitoring, and validation fields can make review work more precise. They do not prove safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, trust, outcomes, or maturity.
