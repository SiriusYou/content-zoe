# Triggers and prioritization

## Why a trigger matters

An AI review queue should explain why an item is in the queue. A trigger prevents the queue from becoming a vague backlog and helps reviewers understand what question must be answered.

The report may describe triggers such as:

- unresolved carry-forward item;
- new or changed use;
- expanded user group or setting;
- changed output type or downstream action;
- source-attribute update;
- monitoring exception;
- near miss, incident, or user concern;
- owner change;
- fallback or contingency gap;
- pending validation;
- retirement, replacement, narrowing, or pause question.

## Prioritization without overclaiming

The report may say that review order can consider impact, workflow dependency, patient-facing action, clinical judgment influence, sensitivity of data, user dependence, frequency of change, monitoring gaps, fallback readiness, and unresolved safety or quality signals.

Do not turn these factors into a universal risk scoring model. The source basis supports prioritization questions, not a settled high-medium-low formula.

## Triage outputs

The queue should lead to a next action: request evidence, confirm owner, review monitoring, validate locally, update source attributes, test fallback, update training, narrow use, pause, retire, reopen review, or escalate for decision.

## Boundary

Prioritization can help route attention. It does not prove that lower-priority items are safe or that higher-priority items are unsafe.
