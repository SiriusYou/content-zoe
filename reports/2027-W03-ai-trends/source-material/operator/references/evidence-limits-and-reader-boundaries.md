# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Evidence posture

The source basis supports a cautious, governance-oriented article about review dates for healthcare AI use. It can explain why lifecycle review, documentation, accountability, monitoring, system management, contingency planning, decision-support visibility, responsible adoption, and bounded device context make indefinite approval risky as a governance assumption.

The source basis does not prove that any named organization has a review-date program. It does not supply local review calendars, owner rosters, committee charters, approval terms, audit trails, utilization data, model-performance measurements, safety outcomes, equity outcomes, legal conclusions, or named implementation proof.

The article should therefore frame review dates as a routing and governance discipline, not as proof that an AI-enabled workflow is safe, compliant, effective, fair, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.

## Required warning posture

Use explicit evidence-limit warnings in both locales. The warnings should say that the article is limited to source-bounded governance, safety-management, health IT, decision-support, responsible-adoption, and bounded device-context material. They should also state that the article does not include local review calendars, committee charters, audit logs, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.

Warning language must be locale-pure. English comments should contain English only. Chinese comments should contain Chinese only.

Warnings should not describe how evidence was prepared. Do not say the article relies on locally written notes or author-prepared summaries. Keep the warning focused on what the source basis can and cannot prove.

## Reader questions the article may answer

- Why is a review date useful after an AI-enabled workflow appears approved or implemented?
- What can become stale after launch or owner assignment?
- What fields make a review date operationally useful?
- What kinds of changes should reopen review?
- Why should expired, unknown, or disputed review status remain visible?
- What can the available governance sources support, and what can they not prove?

## Reader questions the article must not answer

- Which vendor, product, hospital, health system, regulator, or public program has the best review-date process?
- Whether a local review calendar satisfies law, regulation, accreditation, certification, procurement, privacy, civil rights, or professional-liability obligations.
- Whether a specific AI-enabled workflow is safe, compliant, effective, fair, locally validated, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.
- Whether FDA device context applies to every healthcare AI workflow.
- Whether a specific local deployment should continue, narrow, pause, retire, or be used in clinical care.

## Phrasing discipline

Use public-facing terms such as "the source basis", "available governance sources", "review record", "continued-use review", "review date", "change trigger", "use boundary", and "evidence gap".

Avoid language that points to non-reader operational details. The reader should see a healthcare AI governance article.

For `Selected Source Basis`, list public source families by name. Acceptable examples include NIST AI RMF, ONC SAFER guides, ONC decision-support intervention material, WHO, AMA, Joint Commission/CHAI, and bounded FDA AI/ML device context. Do not list local filenames or markdown paths as reader sources.
