# Source attributes and local context

## Why source attributes matter

Source attributes can help explain what evidence is available, what it is about, and what it does not cover. A review date should be able to ask whether those attributes still fit the workflow under review. Vendor materials, public guidance, device-context material, policy documents, monitoring records, and local workflow notes can answer different questions.

The article may say that review records should distinguish external material from local evidence. Vendor evidence may inform the review, but it does not answer every local workflow question. Public governance sources can support disciplined questions, but they do not prove that a local deployment is complete, correct, safe, compliant, effective, fair, mature, or outcome-improving.

## Local context that may change

- clinical or operational setting;
- intended users;
- patient or member population affected by the workflow;
- workflow step where the output is used;
- downstream decision influenced by the output;
- local monitoring responsibility;
- fallback contact;
- owner group;
- escalation route;
- source attributes available to reviewers.

The article should use these examples to explain why a review date can be useful, not to imply that every local record contains these fields.

## Public language guard

When describing evidence limits, use reader-facing terms such as "available governance sources", "the source basis", "local evidence", and "review record". Do not point readers toward non-reader operational details.

When naming the source basis for readers, use public source-family names rather than local filenames. The report should not describe its evidence as locally written notes or author-prepared summaries.
