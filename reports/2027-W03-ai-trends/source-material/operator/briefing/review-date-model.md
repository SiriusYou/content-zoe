# Review-date model

## Core idea

A healthcare AI review date is a visible point when continued use should be reconsidered. It is not just a calendar reminder. It should connect the workflow to the current owner, the reviewed use boundary, monitoring signals, source attributes, fallback planning, and unresolved evidence gaps.

The review date matters because AI-enabled workflows can drift without changing their name. A tool may serve new users, appear in a new setting, influence a different decision, depend on changed vendor material, receive updates, or accumulate monitoring concerns. A review date gives governance teams a recurring question: does the previous decision still fit the current use?

## What a useful review-date record can include

- workflow or AI-enabled function;
- current accountable owner or owner group;
- clinical or operational setting;
- intended users;
- reviewed use boundary;
- last review date, when available;
- next review date, when available;
- review trigger categories;
- monitoring responsibility;
- fallback contact;
- escalation route;
- continued-use decision, when available;
- unresolved evidence gaps.

These fields are useful only when they help route action. A date without a responsible owner, reviewed boundary, or next-action path can become another passive label.

## What a review date is not

A review date is not proof of safety, compliance, effectiveness, fairness, patient trust, continuity, resilience, maturity, or outcome improvement. It is also not a substitute for local evidence, legal analysis, professional judgment, clinical review, validation, procurement review, privacy review, cybersecurity review, or patient-safety review.

The article may argue that a review date makes continued-use questions easier to find and route. It must not claim that the date answers those questions.
