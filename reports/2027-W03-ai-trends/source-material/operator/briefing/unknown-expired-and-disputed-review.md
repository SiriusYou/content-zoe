# Unknown, expired, and disputed review status

## Unknown status

Unknown review status should remain visible. If no one can tell when the workflow was last reviewed, what use boundary was reviewed, or who can decide continued use, the item is not resolved. The next action may be owner confirmation, evidence gathering, boundary clarification, or escalation.

The article should frame unknown status as a governance routing problem, not as proof that the workflow is unsafe or noncompliant.

## Expired status

An expired review date should prompt a continued-use question. The question is not simply whether the date is old. The question is whether the current owner, use boundary, monitoring signals, fallback plan, source attributes, and unresolved evidence gaps still support the current use.

Expired status should not automatically be converted into assumed approval. It should also not automatically be described as harm. The source-bounded claim is narrower: expired status deserves visible review.

## Disputed status

Review status can be disputed when different groups control different parts of a workflow, when evidence is incomplete, when monitoring concerns are unresolved, or when the reviewed use boundary no longer matches practice. A review record should show the dispute and route it to a decision-maker rather than hiding it behind a generic continued-use label.

The article may connect this point to owner-of-record thinking from the prior theme, but it should keep the primary focus on review dates and continued-use decisions.
