# Continued-use decisions

## Review is useful only if it can route a decision

A review date should lead to a visible next step. The step might be to continue current use, continue with conditions, gather missing evidence, narrow use, update user notice, review monitoring, revise fallback planning, escalate for decision, pause use, retire the workflow, or reopen governance review.

The article should emphasize that the date alone is not the decision. The date creates the moment when the organization asks what decision is still justified by the current use boundary and available evidence.

## Decision categories the article may describe

- continue within the reviewed use boundary;
- continue with added monitoring;
- gather evidence before expansion;
- narrow the use boundary;
- update source attributes or user-facing context;
- revise fallback or downtime planning;
- escalate unresolved ownership or evidence gaps;
- pause use pending review;
- retire or archive the workflow;
- reopen governance review.

These are practical routing categories. They should not be described as proof of compliance, safety, effectiveness, fairness, or maturity.

## Expired or unknown review status

An expired review date should not quietly become continued approval. Unknown review status should remain visible as an unresolved governance item. The article can say that visibility matters because it prevents old approval language from carrying more weight than the current evidence supports.

The article should avoid implying that every expired review requires the same response. The safer source-bounded claim is that expired or unknown status should trigger a routing question: who reviews it, what changed, what evidence is missing, and what decision is needed?
