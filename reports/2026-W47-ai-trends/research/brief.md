# Research Brief

## Objective

Produce a source-bounded report on healthcare AI use creep: a tool that was reviewed for one context quietly expands into a materially different one before governance records, training, monitoring, and ownership catch up.

## Core Thesis

The staged material supports a narrow argument:

- Healthcare AI governance should not stop at inventorying a tool.
- The harder problem is controlling expansion across users, settings, populations, data sources, and decision points.
- The same model or feature label can become a different governance question once the workflow and reliance pattern change.

## What The Staged Sources Support

- Approved use should specify intended users, workflow setting, data context, population assumptions, decision point, evidence status, and accountable owner.
- Use creep can happen through general-purpose tools, embedded vendor features, and informal workarounds that bypass formal project intake.
- Pilot-to-production spread and cross-setting spread can change staffing, training, monitoring, escalation, and operational risk even if the tool name does not change.
- Review triggers should include changes in user group, setting, population, data source, interface, vendor release, downstream action, or reliance pattern.
- Training, ownership, and accountability can drift when actual use expands faster than the original launch record.

## What The Staged Sources Do Not Support

- No product ranking, vendor ranking, or named market roundup.
- No claim that any specific organization expanded use safely or unsafely.
- No claim that approved-use records, audits, source attributes, training updates, or change notices prove safety, compliance, fairness, privacy protection, patient trust, outcome improvement, or operational maturity.
- No legal or regulatory opinion for a specific workflow.
- No prevalence claim about shadow use, no local usage-log claim, and no outcome or incident claim.

## Recommended Story Spine

1. Open with the use-creep problem: approval for one context is not approval for adjacent work.
2. Show why inventory alone is insufficient: governance has to record approved use and out-of-scope boundaries.
3. Explain the main spread paths: general-purpose tools, embedded features, pilot-to-production drift, and cross-setting spread.
4. Make scope control operational: review triggers, change notices, local validation, monitoring against actual use, and owner sign-off.
5. Close on the human layer: training, ownership, escalation, and accountability have to move with the real workflow.

## Implementation Notes For Drafting

- Keep the frame on scope control and expansion review, not on whether AI is broadly good or bad.
- Use concrete transition examples such as drafting to decision support, one clinic to another, one role to another, or adult to specialty/pediatric context.
- Treat approved-use records and source attributes as review aids, not proof.
- Keep the tone evidence-cautious and operational.
- Include a short `Selected Source Basis` section in each locale.

## Mandatory Evidence Warnings

Use locale-specific `EVIDENCE_GRADE_WARN` comments in the final report files.

- Leading warning: the draft is limited to staged intended-use, decision-support, risk-management, governance, and professional-source material, and excludes local usage logs, user-behavior evidence, outcome data, legal analysis, and named implementation proof.
- Mid-report warning: approved-use records, expansion review, monitoring, change notices, and training updates can make use creep more reviewable, but they do not prove safety, compliance, fairness, privacy protection, patient trust, outcome improvement, or operational maturity.

## Title Direction

Prefer a title that foregrounds approved use, scope expansion, or review triggers without naming a week key.

- Healthcare AI Needs an Expansion Review Line
- Approved for One Use Is Not Approved for the Next
- Healthcare AI Has a Scope-Creep Problem

## Selected Source Basis

- NIST AI RMF and the Generative AI Profile for context mapping, lifecycle monitoring, and role/accountability framing.
- FDA CDS materials for intended-use discipline and user-context questions.
- ONC decision-support intervention materials for source-attribute visibility in covered contexts.
- WHO, AMA, and Joint Commission/CHAI references for accountability, human oversight, locally adapted governance, and responsible adoption posture.

## Source Gaps To State Plainly

- No local implementation evidence.
- No local registry or monitoring evidence.
- No user interviews or training-comprehension evidence.
- No safety, outcome, or operational-performance evidence.
- No legal analysis and no named organization proof.
