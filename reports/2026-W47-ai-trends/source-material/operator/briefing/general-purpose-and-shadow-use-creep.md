# General-purpose and shadow use creep

## Editorial use

Use this file to address use creep that happens outside formal AI project channels. The report should cover general-purpose tools, productivity assistants, vendor-embedded AI, and individual workarounds that begin shaping healthcare work before formal governance records catch up.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile supports attention to risks that are shaped by system context, downstream use, human interaction, and organizational controls.
- WHO statement on safe and ethical AI for health calls for caution, governance, and evidence before widespread routine use of large language model tools in health contexts.
- AMA materials discuss augmented intelligence across clinical and nonclinical medical settings and emphasize practical guidance, transparency, privacy, cybersecurity, and accountability.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement supports locally adapted governance for responsible AI adoption.

## Reader-safe claims

- General-purpose tools can move from harmless drafting support into documentation, patient communication, coding, scheduling, triage, or operational decision support.
- Shadow use can bypass approved-use records because the tool is not introduced as a formal healthcare AI project.
- Governance should ask what work the tool is actually shaping, not only what category the tool claims to occupy.
- Detection can include procurement review, product-update review, staff reporting routes, workflow observation, access review, and periodic inventory refresh.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim all general-purpose AI use in healthcare is inappropriate.
- Do not claim any specific product or vendor is safe or unsafe.
- Do not claim informal use prevalence without staged evidence.
- Do not imply governance can or should eliminate every individual workaround.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support caution and governance questions for general-purpose and embedded AI. They do not include local shadow-use measurements, incident data, or product-specific evidence.
