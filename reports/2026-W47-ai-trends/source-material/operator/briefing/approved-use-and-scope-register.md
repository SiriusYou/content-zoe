# Approved use and scope register

## Editorial use

Use this file to make W47 about approved-use boundaries. The report should argue that a healthcare AI record should not stop at "this tool exists." It should name the approved use, intended users, workflow setting, patient or operational population, data context, decision point, evidence status, and owner responsible for expansion review.

## Source basis

- NIST AI RMF supports mapping the context, actors, uses, benefits, and risks of an AI system before managing risk.
- FDA Clinical Decision Support Software materials emphasize intended use, user context, and whether software informs rather than replaces professional judgment.
- ONC DSI materials support source-attribute visibility and structured information about decision support interventions in covered certified-health-IT contexts.
- WHO health AI guidance supports responsibility, accountability, transparency, and human oversight.

## Reader-safe claims

- A scope register should answer what the tool is approved to do, for whom, in which setting, using which data, and under whose ownership.
- The register should also say what is outside scope: unreviewed populations, settings, users, data sources, decision points, or downstream actions.
- Scope boundaries are useful only if someone monitors whether actual use still matches the approved record.
- Records should be updated when a tool is repurposed, embedded in a new workflow, or used by a new role.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim a register proves governance maturity.
- Do not imply every scope change is unsafe.
- Do not claim all AI use contexts are equally sensitive or equally regulated.
- Do not give legal classifications for any specific workflow.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support scope-record questions. They do not include local registry evidence, usage logs, user interviews, safety outcomes, or legal determinations.
