# Pilot-to-production and cross-setting spread

## Editorial use

Use this file to cover the common drift from pilot to production and from one setting to another. The report should explain that spread can change the governance question even if the model or vendor feature name stays the same.

## Source basis

- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement supports policies, local validation, monitoring, and use adapted to organizational context.
- NIST AI RMF supports lifecycle risk management and context-specific mapping.
- AMA augmented-intelligence materials connect AI use with workflow, risk, training, and professional responsibility.
- WHO health AI guidance supports context-sensitive deployment and protection of affected people.

## Reader-safe claims

- Moving from pilot to production can change staffing, workflow pressure, user mix, training needs, monitoring burden, and escalation expectations.
- Moving from one department or site to another can change patient population, documentation habits, data quality, queue design, and local ownership.
- Expansion should trigger review of evidence, workflow fit, monitoring plan, training, fallback, and escalation routes.
- A tool can retain the same label while becoming a different operational risk because the setting and reliance pattern changed.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim pilots never generalize.
- Do not claim production expansion is unsafe by default.
- Do not infer that a named organization has succeeded or failed at scaling.
- Do not treat one local validation as proof for every future context.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support review questions about spread and local context. They do not prove that any pilot, site transfer, or production expansion worked or failed.
