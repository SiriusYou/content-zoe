# Training, ownership, and accountability drift

## Editorial use

Use this file to keep the human side of use creep visible. Scope expansion can make training, ownership, escalation, and accountability drift apart from the actual work.

## Source basis

- AMA augmented-intelligence materials connect responsible AI use with workflow, risk, training, privacy, cybersecurity, and professional responsibility.
- WHO health AI guidance supports responsibility, accountability, human autonomy, and protection of affected people.
- NIST AI RMF supports roles, responsibilities, governance, and risk management across lifecycle contexts.
- Joint Commission/CHAI responsible-use guidance announcement supports policies, local validation, monitoring, and use in context.

## Reader-safe claims

- When an AI-enabled workflow expands, the original training may no longer match the users, setting, or decisions now affected.
- Ownership can drift if the sponsor, product owner, clinical owner, data owner, or escalation path remains tied to the original pilot while actual use spreads.
- Training updates should cover intended use, limits, review expectations, escalation, fallback, and what changed since the prior approval.
- Accountability should follow actual use, not only the original launch record.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim training guarantees safe use.
- Do not imply professional responsibility alone solves workflow or vendor risk.
- Do not claim every user group needs the same training or approval route.
- Do not claim expanded use is mature because an owner is named.

## Evidence limit

The staged sources support training, ownership, and accountability questions. They do not prove that any local training or ownership model is adequate.
