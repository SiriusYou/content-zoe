# W36 topic and source boundary

Job: 2026-W36-ai-trends
Topic: AI in healthcare - weekly
Week identity: 2026-W36
Prepared: 2026-05-25

## Intended angle

This source set supports a broad healthcare-AI weekly report for a content product. The strongest angle is operational proof: healthcare AI is entering a phase where responsible adoption depends on what organizations can inspect, monitor, document, and learn from, not only on adoption interest or guidance language.

The report title should be a reader-facing topic title, not a job label. Do not include `2026-W36` or any other week key in the H1 title. Week identity belongs in job metadata and README, not in the article headline. Also avoid repeating recent title frames; use a fresh title construction.

## Source classes included

- Health-system assurance, governance-body, patient-safety, and evidence-operations references.
- Algorithm transparency, decision-support source attributes, interoperability, TEFCA, and HHS AI strategy references.
- FDA AI-enabled device software, predetermined change control, lifecycle management, and monitoring references.
- Third-party AI risk, vendor evidence, cybersecurity, supply-chain transparency, and operational-resilience references.
- Physician experience, human accountability, privacy, de-identification, WHO, OECD, and NIST governance references.

## Source classes not included

- No private project documents.
- No prior-week source bundles.
- No patient-level data.
- No private vendor diligence.
- No direct clinical outcome dataset.
- No comprehensive funding, litigation, market-share, or M&A map.

## Claims that are safe to make

- Healthcare AI assurance needs evidence review, monitoring, safety learning, accountable ownership, and reviewable surfaces.
- Transparency fields, source attributes, and interoperability can improve inspection, while not proving performance.
- FDA device software materials illustrate disciplined lifecycle control and bounded change, while not applying unchanged to every workflow AI tool.
- Third-party AI risk and cybersecurity controls are part of healthcare AI governance because vendor systems shape evidence access, contracts, data flows, and incident response.
- Physician AI exposure supports training, escalation, documentation, and human-accountability pressure.
- Privacy, de-identification, WHO, OECD, and NIST references set boundaries and vocabulary, not proof of local readiness.

## Claims that are not safe to make from this bundle

- That AI broadly improves patient outcomes or lowers costs.
- That assurance guidance proves safe implementation, compliance, fairness, ROI, or maturity.
- That transparency metadata proves model performance.
- That FDA device software discipline validates non-device workflow AI.
- That vendor evidence, contract language, or framework adoption proves resilience.
- That physician use proves audited deployment quality.

## Publication gate guidance

Keep evidence warnings visible. Reject or restage if the generated report overstates clinical outcomes, deployment maturity, regulatory validation, vendor performance, cybersecurity readiness, privacy compliance, or assurance maturity. Also reject or edit if the H1 title includes a week key such as `2026-W36`, or if it repeats recent title framing instead of using a fresh construction.
