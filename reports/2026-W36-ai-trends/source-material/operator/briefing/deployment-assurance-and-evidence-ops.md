## Facts and observations

- Assurance materials support a practical governance frame: healthcare AI needs evidence review, monitoring, risk assessment, and accountable ownership after selection.
- Patient-safety resources support treating AI incidents, near misses, and workflow failure modes as safety-learning inputs.
- Health-system guidance supports governance bodies and operating routines rather than symbolic policy statements.
- These sources support a report about assurance becoming operational infrastructure.
- They do not prove that any specific health system has achieved mature AI assurance, measured clinical benefit, or deployment readiness.

## W36 framing

- Reader-safe headline direction: healthcare AI is moving from assurance language toward operational proof.
- Connect assurance to monitoring, safety learning, evidence review, and responsible ownership.
- Avoid claiming that standards or guidance documents prove implementation maturity.

## Evidence limits

- These are governance and safety references, not comparative implementation audits.
- They support requirements for assurance work, not measured patient outcomes, ROI, or broad deployment success.
