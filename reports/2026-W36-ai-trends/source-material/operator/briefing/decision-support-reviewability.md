## Facts and observations

- Algorithm transparency and decision-support materials support a reviewability frame: users and governance teams need source attributes, quality information, and workflow context.
- TEFCA and interoperability references support the importance of data visibility and exchange infrastructure.
- HHS AI strategy materials support responsible AI as an enterprise governance problem, not only a tool-by-tool procurement issue.
- These references support the claim that reviewable surfaces are becoming part of healthcare AI operations.
- They do not prove that transparency metadata establishes accuracy, fairness, safety, or local readiness.

## W36 framing

- Reader-safe headline direction: documentation and source attributes are becoming inspection tools.
- Connect algorithm transparency, decision support, and interoperability to practical review.
- Avoid treating transparency fields as substitutes for validation or local monitoring.

## Evidence limits

- These sources describe policy direction and review surfaces.
- They do not validate a specific model, product, vendor, workflow, or patient outcome.
