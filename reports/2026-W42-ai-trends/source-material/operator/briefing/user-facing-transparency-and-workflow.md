# User-facing transparency and workflow context

## Editorial use

Use this file to frame transparency as a workflow question. The reader-facing report should emphasize that an AI-enabled healthcare tool is not transparent merely because it has a technical label or policy reference. It becomes more usable when the right people can understand what the system is for, where it fits in a workflow, what inputs and outputs mean, when human judgment remains necessary, and what limitations or gaps are known.

## Source basis to cite cautiously

- FDA/Health Canada/MHRA transparency guiding principles for ML-enabled medical devices say transparency depends on the audience, context of use, timing, placement, and communication method.
- The same guidance links transparency to intended use, intended users, use environment, target populations, inputs, outputs, benefits, risks, performance information, known limitations, and lifecycle maintenance.
- The guidance also emphasizes human-centered design and relevant audiences including healthcare professionals, patients, caregivers, administrators, payors, governing bodies, and others involved in decisions about the device.
- WHO ethics and governance guidance supports transparency, explainability, accountability, inclusion, responsiveness, and human rights as AI-for-health governance principles.

## Supported claims

- Useful transparency should be audience-specific: a clinician, patient, IT leader, compliance officer, and procurement team may need different levels of detail.
- Workflow context matters. A tool that informs a clinician's judgment creates different transparency needs from a tool that automates a downstream operational step.
- Known gaps and limitations are part of transparency, not a failure of transparency.
- Human-centered design can help identify what information users need before, during, and after use.

## Unsafe claims

- Do not claim transparency alone makes a tool safe, fair, effective, or trustworthy.
- Do not claim patient comprehension or clinician adoption improves unless staged evidence directly supports that.
- Do not imply FDA transparency principles apply as binding rules to every healthcare AI tool.

## Evidence warning

The staged sources support transparency as a governance and communication duty. They do not provide deployment evidence showing that a particular transparency artifact changed clinical behavior, reduced harm, improved outcomes, or increased patient trust.
