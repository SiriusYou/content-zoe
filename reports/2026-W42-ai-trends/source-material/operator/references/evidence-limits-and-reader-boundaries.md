# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Editorial use

Use this reference to keep the W42 report cautious. The report should explain what the staged sources support and what they do not support.

## What the sources support

- Transparency is a lifecycle and audience-specific governance duty.
- Source attributes, public lists, labels, and summaries can help users and governance teams ask better questions.
- Certified-health-IT transparency requirements and FDA device guidance apply in specific regulatory/product contexts; they should not be generalized to every healthcare AI tool.
- Local implementation context remains necessary even when national guidance exists.
- Known gaps, unavailable information, and limitations should be reported rather than hidden.

## What the sources do not support

- No claim that transparency proves safety, effectiveness, fairness, cybersecurity readiness, privacy safety, compliance, clinical benefit, or patient trust.
- No claim that every healthcare AI tool is covered by FDA device guidance or ONC HTI-1.
- No claim that any specific hospital, vendor, or product has succeeded or failed.
- No claim that public AI-device lists are comprehensive or that public summaries are complete.
- No claim that a source-attribute record or assurance checklist proves local performance.

## Required evidence warnings

The final report should preserve two evidence-grade warnings:

1. A leading warning that the draft is limited to staged governance and transparency source material and does not include local deployment evidence, outcome data, or named case proof.
2. A mid-report warning around source attributes/device lists/assurance records noting that transparency artifacts are not proof of local performance or safety.

## Reader promise

The report may offer a governance frame: "Healthcare AI needs transparency people can use." It must not offer a factual weekly market roundup, vendor ranking, or case-specific performance claim unless new staged source material is added.
