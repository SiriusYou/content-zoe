# Source attributes and local evidence

## Why source attributes matter

An uncertainty label is more useful when it identifies the kind of evidence the output relies on. The evidence may include public governance sources, local workflow documentation, decision-support configuration, monitoring records, validation work, or review notes. The article should distinguish between public source families and local evidence the article does not have.

The source basis can support questions about documentation, monitoring, human oversight, risk management, workflow fit, and responsible adoption. It cannot prove that a local output is calibrated, current, complete, safe, compliant, fair, effective, trusted, or outcome-improving.

## Local evidence that may be needed

- validation results;
- calibration or performance checks;
- data freshness;
- workflow fit review;
- monitoring records;
- override or non-use patterns;
- user feedback;
- owner review;
- incident or near-miss review;
- fallback route testing.

The article may name these as examples of local evidence. It must not imply that the article possesses them.

## Public language guard

When describing evidence limits, use reader-facing terms such as "available governance sources", "the source basis", "local evidence", and "review record". Do not point readers toward internal preparation details.
