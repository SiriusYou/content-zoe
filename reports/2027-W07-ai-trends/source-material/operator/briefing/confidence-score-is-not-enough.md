# A confidence score is not enough

## Why a number can mislead

A numerical confidence score can look authoritative even when readers do not know what it means, what data shaped it, what context is missing, or whether the workflow has changed since review. The article may say that confidence information needs context to be usable.

The article should not claim that all scores are misleading or that every workflow needs the same display. The source-bounded claim is narrower: uncertainty communication should help the intended reader understand limits, not merely show a number.

## Questions a usable confidence signal should answer

- What is the output trying to support?
- Who is expected to read it?
- What evidence or source family supports the output?
- What important context may be missing?
- What condition should trigger human review?
- What fallback route exists if the output is not usable?
- Who owns follow-up when uncertainty is material?

## Boundary

Do not invent calibration results, thresholds, validation studies, measured outcomes, or local performance claims. The article can discuss why those facts matter without pretending to have them.
