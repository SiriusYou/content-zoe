# Evidence limits and reader boundaries

## Evidence posture

The source basis supports a cautious, governance-oriented article about uncertainty communication in healthcare AI workflows. It can explain why transparency, documentation, workflow fit, human oversight, monitoring, contingency planning, decision-support visibility, responsible adoption, and bounded device context make uncertainty labels useful.

The source basis does not prove that any named organization has an uncertainty-label process. It does not supply local model-performance results, calibration curves, confidence thresholds, validation reports, user studies, patient outcomes, owner rosters, committee charters, audit trails, legal conclusions, or named implementation proof.

The article should therefore frame uncertainty labels as a way to make limits and review needs visible, not as proof that an AI-enabled workflow is safe, unsafe, compliant, noncompliant, effective, ineffective, fair, unfair, trusted, untrusted, mature, or outcome-changing.

## Required warning posture

Use explicit evidence-limit warnings in both locales. The warnings should say that the article is limited to source-bounded governance, health IT, decision-support, workflow documentation, safety-management, responsible-adoption, and bounded device-context material. They should also state that the article does not include local calibration results, model-performance results, confidence thresholds, validation reports, legal analysis, measured outcomes, or named implementation proof.

Warning language must be locale-pure. English comments should contain English only. Chinese comments should contain Chinese only.

Warnings should not describe how evidence was prepared. Keep the warning focused on what the source basis can and cannot prove.

## Reader questions the article may answer

- Why can healthcare AI outputs look more certain than the evidence supports?
- What should an uncertainty label tell the reader?
- Why is a confidence score alone often insufficient?
- What missing context should trigger review?
- How do fallback route, escalation route, owner group, and review trigger fit together?
- What can the available governance sources support, and what can they not prove?

## Reader questions the article must not answer

- Which vendor, product, hospital, health system, regulator, or public program has the best uncertainty-label process?
- Whether a local uncertainty label satisfies law, regulation, accreditation, certification, procurement, privacy, civil rights, labor, or professional-liability obligations.
- Whether a specific AI-enabled workflow is safe, unsafe, compliant, noncompliant, effective, fair, locally validated, trusted, resilient, mature, or outcome-improving.
- Whether FDA device context applies to every healthcare AI workflow.
- Whether a specific output should be used, ignored, escalated, overridden, disclosed, or acted on in clinical care.

## Phrasing discipline

Use public-facing terms such as "the source basis", "available governance sources", "uncertainty label", "confidence qualifier", "missing context", "review trigger", "fallback route", "escalation route", "owner group", "human review", and "evidence gap".

Avoid language that points to non-reader operational details. The reader should see a healthcare AI governance article.

For `Selected Source Basis`, list public source families by name. Acceptable examples include NIST AI RMF, ONC SAFER guides, ONC decision-support intervention material, WHO, AMA, Joint Commission/CHAI, and bounded FDA AI/ML device context.
